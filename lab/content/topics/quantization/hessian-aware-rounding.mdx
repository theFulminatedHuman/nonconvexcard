<Intuition>
Round-to-nearest treats each weight as if it acted alone. It does not. If two input features are
correlated, rounding one weight up can be compensated by nudging its partner down, leaving the
layer's output almost unchanged. The nearest grid point is the right answer for the *weight*; it is
the wrong answer for the *function the layer computes*.

So quantize the weights one at a time, and after each one, solve exactly for the best correction to
everything not yet quantized. That correction has a closed form, and computing it is the whole
algorithm.
</Intuition>

## The layer-wise objective

Post-training quantization methods almost never optimise the end-to-end loss — that would need
labels, backpropagation through the whole network, and far more compute. Instead they fix each layer
in turn and ask that its *output* be preserved on a small calibration set.

<Claim kind="proposition" id="layerwise-objective" title="The layer-wise objective is a quadratic form in the weight error">
Let a linear layer have weights $W \in \R^{m \times d}$ and let $X \in \R^{d \times n}$ hold $n$
calibration inputs as columns. Then
$$
\big\|WX - \widehat W X\big\|_F^2 \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{m} \delta_i^\top \big(XX^\top\big)\, \delta_i,
\qquad \delta_i = \big(\widehat W - W\big)_{i,:}^\top ,
$$
so the problem decouples across the $m$ output rows, each governed by the same matrix
$H := 2XX^\top$, the Hessian of the row objective.
</Claim>

<Proof>
The squared Frobenius norm sums over rows:
$\|WX - \widehat WX\|_F^2 = \sum_i \|(W-\widehat W)_{i,:}X\|_2^2$. For a single row, writing
$\delta = (\widehat W - W)_{i,:}^\top \in \R^d$,
$$
\|\delta^\top X\|_2^2 = \delta^\top X X^\top \delta .
$$
Rows share $X$ and appear in no common term, so the objective separates. Writing the row objective as
$f(\delta) = \delta^\top XX^\top \delta$, its Hessian is $\nabla^2 f = 2XX^\top = H$, constant in
$\delta$ because $f$ is exactly quadratic. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Note>
$H = 2XX^\top$ is positive semidefinite but singular whenever $n &lt; d$ or the calibration inputs are
linearly dependent — routine in practice. Implementations add a damping term $H + \lambda I$ with
$\lambda$ a small multiple of $\operatorname{tr}(H)/d$. This is ridge regularisation of the
correction step, and it is a numerical necessity rather than a modelling choice; the algorithm below
assumes $H \succ 0$ after damping.
</Note>

## The optimal correction

<Claim kind="theorem" id="obs-update" title="Optimal update under a fixed rounding">
Let $H \succ 0$, let $w \in \R^d$ be the current row, and fix coordinate $q$ to the quantized value
$\operatorname{quant}(w_q)$. Among all $\delta \in \R^d$ satisfying that constraint, the one
minimising $\tfrac12\delta^\top H \delta$ is
$$
\boxed{\;\delta^\star \;=\; -\,\frac{w_q - \operatorname{quant}(w_q)}{\big[H^{-1}\big]_{qq}}\;H^{-1}e_q\;}
$$
where $e_q$ is the $q$-th standard basis vector.
</Claim>

<Assumptions>
$H \succ 0$, so the objective is strictly convex and $H^{-1}$ exists; the constraint is the single
linear equation $e_q^\top \delta = \operatorname{quant}(w_q) - w_q$. No assumption is made about the
other coordinates — they are all free to move, which is precisely the point.
</Assumptions>

<Derivation>
<Step why="A strictly convex quadratic under one linear equality constraint; KKT reduces to stationarity of the Lagrangian.">
Write $c = \operatorname{quant}(w_q) - w_q$ and form
$$
\mathcal L(\delta,\lambda) = \tfrac12\delta^\top H\delta + \lambda\big(e_q^\top\delta - c\big).
$$
</Step>
<Step why="Stationarity in δ.">
$\nabla_\delta \mathcal L = H\delta + \lambda e_q = 0 \implies \delta = -\lambda H^{-1}e_q$.
</Step>
<Step why="Impose the constraint to identify the multiplier.">
$e_q^\top \delta = -\lambda\, e_q^\top H^{-1} e_q = -\lambda\big[H^{-1}\big]_{qq} = c$, hence
$\lambda = -c/[H^{-1}]_{qq}$, which is well defined because $[H^{-1}]_{qq} &gt; 0$ for $H \succ 0$.
</Step>
<Step why="Substitute back, and rewrite c in terms of the rounding residual.">
$$
\delta^\star = \frac{c}{[H^{-1}]_{qq}}H^{-1}e_q
= -\frac{w_q - \operatorname{quant}(w_q)}{[H^{-1}]_{qq}}H^{-1}e_q .
$$
Strict convexity makes this stationary point the unique global minimiser. $\blacksquare$
</Step>
</Derivation>

<KeyIdea>
Read the formula: $H^{-1}e_q$ is the $q$-th column of the inverse Hessian, so the correction spreads
over **every** remaining coordinate, in proportion to how strongly each is coupled to $q$ through the
input covariance. Round-to-nearest is the special case where you refuse to apply this correction at
all.
</KeyIdea>

<Claim kind="corollary" id="obs-cost" title="The cost of quantizing a coordinate">
The minimum objective value incurred by fixing coordinate $q$ is
$$
\mathcal E_q \;=\; \tfrac12 (\delta^\star)^\top H \delta^\star
\;=\; \frac{\big(w_q - \operatorname{quant}(w_q)\big)^2}{2\big[H^{-1}\big]_{qq}} .
$$
</Claim>

<Proof>
With $\delta^\star = -\lambda H^{-1}e_q$,
$$
\tfrac12(\delta^\star)^\top H\delta^\star = \tfrac12\lambda^2\, e_q^\top H^{-1}HH^{-1}e_q
= \tfrac12\lambda^2 \big[H^{-1}\big]_{qq},
$$
and substituting $\lambda = -\big(w_q-\operatorname{quant}(w_q)\big)/[H^{-1}]_{qq}$ gives the
result. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

So the greedy rule is: quantize next the coordinate with the smallest
$(w_q - \operatorname{quant}(w_q))^2 / [H^{-1}]_{qq}$ — small rounding residual, or a direction the
data barely constrains.

## Doing it for the whole row without $d$ inversions

Naively, after fixing $q$ you would re-invert the Hessian restricted to the remaining coordinates.
That would be $O(d^4)$ per row. It is unnecessary.

<Claim kind="lemma" id="inverse-deletion" title="Inverse Hessian after deleting a coordinate">
Let $H \succ 0$ and let $F$ index its coordinates with $q \in F$. Writing $H_{-q}$ for $H$ with row
and column $q$ removed,
$$
\big(H_{-q}\big)^{-1} \;=\; \Big[H^{-1} - \frac{1}{[H^{-1}]_{qq}}\,H^{-1}e_q\,e_q^\top H^{-1}\Big]_{-q},
$$
a rank-one correction followed by deletion.
</Claim>

<Proof>
Permute so $q$ is last and write in blocks
$$
H = \begin{pmatrix} A & b \\ b^\top & c\end{pmatrix},
\qquad A = H_{-q}.
$$
The standard block inverse, with Schur complement $s = c - b^\top A^{-1}b &gt; 0$, is
$$
H^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix}
A^{-1} + \dfrac{A^{-1}bb^\top A^{-1}}{s} & -\dfrac{A^{-1}b}{s}\\[6pt]
-\dfrac{b^\top A^{-1}}{s} & \dfrac{1}{s}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Hence $[H^{-1}]_{qq} = 1/s$ and the leading block of $H^{-1}e_q$ is $-A^{-1}b/s$. Therefore
$$
\frac{1}{[H^{-1}]_{qq}}\big[H^{-1}e_q\big]\big[e_q^\top H^{-1}\big]
\Big|_{\text{leading block}}
= s\cdot\frac{A^{-1}b}{s}\cdot\frac{b^\top A^{-1}}{s} = \frac{A^{-1}bb^\top A^{-1}}{s},
$$
which is exactly the correction that must be subtracted from the leading block of $H^{-1}$ to leave
$A^{-1}$. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

<Algorithm title="Hessian-aware rounding of one layer">
1. Accumulate $H = 2XX^\top$ over the calibration set and damp: $H \leftarrow H + \lambda I$.
2. Compute $H^{-1}$ once — **shared by all $m$ rows**, since the proposition showed they share $H$.
3. For $q = 1,\dots,d$ in a fixed order, and for all rows in parallel:
   a. Set $\widehat w_q = \operatorname{quant}(w_q)$.
   b. Apply $\delta^\star$ to the not-yet-quantized coordinates.
   c. Update $H^{-1}$ by the rank-one deletion above.
4. Emit the quantized weights and the per-group scales.
</Algorithm>

<Details summary="Why a fixed order, and why Cholesky">
The greedy order of the corollary is the locally optimal choice, but it makes different rows quantize
coordinates in different sequences, which destroys the shared-$H^{-1}$ structure and with it any hope
of using the GPU efficiently. The empirical finding behind GPTQ is that for large layers a fixed
order costs very little accuracy while allowing every row to be processed simultaneously — an
engineering trade justified by measurement, not by a theorem.

With a fixed order the sequence of rank-one deletions is known in advance, and the required columns
of the successive inverses are exactly the rows of the Cholesky factor of $H^{-1}$. Computing that
factorisation once, in double precision, replaces $d$ explicit rank-one updates whose errors
accumulate — the repeated updates are numerically fragile because $H$ is ill-conditioned after
damping.
</Details>

## Is the correction actually needed?

<Claim kind="proposition" id="rtn-suboptimal" title="Round-to-nearest is suboptimal whenever inputs are correlated">
Suppose $H$ is not diagonal. Then there exist a weight vector $w$ and a quantization grid for which
round-to-nearest incurs strictly larger layer-wise error than the corrected procedure.
</Claim>

<Proof>
Take $d = 2$ with
$$
H = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \rho \\ \rho & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad 0 &lt; |\rho| &lt; 1,
$$
which is positive definite. Let the grid be the integers and take $w = (\tfrac12 - \eps,\ 0)$ for
small $\eps &gt; 0$, so that $\operatorname{quant}(w_1) = 0$ and $w_2 = 0$ is already on the grid.

Round-to-nearest produces $\widehat w = (0,0)$, hence $\delta^{\mathrm{RTN}} = -w$ and
$$
\mathcal E^{\mathrm{RTN}} = \tfrac12 \delta^\top H \delta = \tfrac12\big(\tfrac12-\eps\big)^2 .
$$

The corrected procedure fixes coordinate 1 to $0$ and then chooses $\delta_2$ freely, minimising
$$
g(\delta_2) = \tfrac12\Big[\big(\tfrac12-\eps\big)^2 - 2\rho\big(\tfrac12-\eps\big)\delta_2 + \delta_2^2\Big]
$$
— note $\delta_1 = -(\tfrac12-\eps)$ is forced. Setting $g'(\delta_2)=0$ gives
$\delta_2^\star = \rho(\tfrac12-\eps)$ and
$$
\mathcal E^{\mathrm{OBS}} = \tfrac12\big(\tfrac12-\eps\big)^2\big(1-\rho^2\big)
\;&lt;\; \mathcal E^{\mathrm{RTN}},
$$
strictly, since $0 &lt; 1-\rho^2 &lt; 1$. $\blacksquare$
</Proof>

The gap is governed by $1-\rho^2$: the more correlated the inputs, the more there is to gain. Since
the input activations of a transformer layer are strongly correlated across channels, this is the
regime that applies.

<NotSaid>
The objective optimised here is *layer-wise output reconstruction on a calibration set*, not the
model's loss. Three gaps follow, and they are all real:
- **Error accumulates across layers.** Each layer is optimised assuming its input is the
  *unquantized* model's input; once earlier layers are quantized, that assumption is false.
  Sequential variants that feed quantized activations forward address this partially.
- **Calibration set dependence.** $H$ is estimated from a few hundred sequences; a distribution shift
  at deployment invalidates it, and nothing in the derivation bounds that.
- **Reconstruction is a proxy.** Nothing here shows small layer-wise MSE implies small degradation in
  generation quality. The literature reports that it usually does; that is an empirical regularity,
  not a consequence of any of the results above.
</NotSaid>
