## The model

<Definition id="rag-marginal" title="RAG as marginalisation over a latent document">
Let $x$ be a query, $y$ a response, and $z$ a document from a corpus $\Zcal$. Treat $z$ as a latent
variable:
$$
p(y\mid x) = \sum_{z\in\Zcal} \underbrace{p_\eta(z\mid x)}_{\text{retriever}}\ \underbrace{p_\theta(y\mid x,z)}_{\text{generator}} .
$$
In practice the sum is truncated to the top-$k$ retrieved documents, with the retriever posterior
$$
p_\eta(z\mid x) = \frac{\exp\big(\ip{f_\eta(x)}{g_\eta(z)}\big)}{\sum_{z'\in\mathrm{top}\text{-}k}\exp\big(\ip{f_\eta(x)}{g_\eta(z')}\big)} .
$$
</Definition>

<Intuition>
The whole of RAG is one line of probability: the answer depends on a document you do not observe, so
sum over the possibilities weighted by how likely each is. Everything that distinguishes RAG systems —
chunking, hybrid search, reranking, query rewriting, iterative retrieval — is a choice about how to
construct or sharpen $p_\eta(z\mid x)$, not a change to this equation.
</Intuition>

<Proposition id="rag-sequence-vs-token" title="RAG-sequence and RAG-token are different models">
$$
p_{\text{seq}}(y\mid x) = \sum_z p_\eta(z\mid x)\prod_{t=1}^{|y|}p_\theta(y_t\mid x,z,y_{<t}),
\qquad
p_{\text{tok}}(y\mid x) = \prod_{t=1}^{|y|}\sum_z p_\eta(z\mid x)\,p_\theta(y_t\mid x,z,y_{<t}).
$$
These are not equal: the first is a mixture of products and the second a product of mixtures.
$p_{\text{seq}}$ commits to a single document for the whole response; $p_{\text{tok}}$ can draw
different tokens from different documents.
</Proposition>

<Proof>
A sum of products need not equal the product of the sums. Concretely, take $|y|=2$, two documents
$z_1,z_2$ with equal weight $1/2$, and generators that are deterministic and disagree:
$p_\theta(\cdot\mid z_1)$ puts mass $1$ on $y = (a,a)$ and $p_\theta(\cdot\mid z_2)$ on $y = (b,b)$.
Then $p_{\text{seq}}((a,b)) = 0$, while
$p_{\text{tok}}((a,b)) = \tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12 = \tfrac14$. The token-level model assigns positive
probability to a response that no single document supports.
</Proof>

<Note title="The trade-off this exposes">
$p_{\text{tok}}$ can synthesise across documents, which is necessary for multi-hop questions.
$p_{\text{seq}}$ cannot, but for that reason every generated response is attributable to exactly one
source — which is what a citation-bearing system needs. The example in the proof is also a warning:
token-level mixing can produce a fluent response that is inconsistent with every individual document,
which is one precise mechanism by which a grounded system can still be wrong.

Fusion-in-Decoder takes a third route: encode each document separately and let the decoder attend
over all of them jointly, so the mixing happens inside attention rather than in an explicit
marginalisation.
</Note>

## A variational view

<Theorem id="rag-elbo" title="A lower bound on the RAG log-likelihood">
For any distribution $q(z)$ over documents,
$$
\log p(y\mid x) \;\ge\; \E_{z\sim q}\big[\log p_\theta(y\mid x,z)\big] - \mathrm{KL}\big(q(z)\,\|\,p_\eta(z\mid x)\big),
$$
with equality if and only if $q(z) = p(z\mid x,y)$, the true posterior given both query and answer.
</Theorem>

<Proof>
Write the marginal as an expectation under $q$ and apply Jensen:
$$
\log\sum_zp_\eta(z\mid x)p_\theta(y\mid x,z)
= \log\E_{z\sim q}\left[\frac{p_\eta(z\mid x)p_\theta(y\mid x,z)}{q(z)}\right]
\ge \E_{z\sim q}\log\frac{p_\eta(z\mid x)p_\theta(y\mid x,z)}{q(z)} ,
$$
and the right-hand side rearranges into the stated form. The Jensen gap is exactly
$\mathrm{KL}\big(q(z)\|p(z\mid x,y)\big)$, which vanishes iff $q$ is the true posterior.
</Proof>

<KeyIdea>
The bound identifies what a retriever *should* approximate: not $p(z\mid x)$, the documents relevant
to the query, but $p(z\mid x,y)$, the documents that explain the answer. Those differ, and the gap is
the reason two-stage training helps — the retriever trained on query–document relevance is optimising
the wrong posterior, and methods that distil the generator's likelihood back into the retriever
(REALM, and the retriever-update schemes that follow it) are closing exactly this gap.
</KeyIdea>

## Error propagation

<Theorem id="error-propagation" title="Retrieval error propagates into generation">
Fix a query $x$ and suppose there is a set $Z^\star$ of documents sufficient to answer it. Let
$$
\gamma = p_\eta\big(Z^\star \mid x\big)
$$
be the retriever's probability of supplying a sufficient document (its recall, in probability form).
Let $p_{\mathrm{RAG}}(\cdot\mid x)$ be the RAG output distribution and $p_{\mathrm{oracle}}(\cdot\mid x)$
the distribution obtained by conditioning on $Z^\star$ with certainty. Then
$$
\big\| p_{\mathrm{RAG}}(\cdot\mid x) - p_{\mathrm{oracle}}(\cdot\mid x)\big\|_{\mathrm{TV}} \;\le\; 1-\gamma .
$$
</Theorem>

<Proof>
Write $A = Z^\star$ and decompose the mixture:
$$
p_{\mathrm{RAG}}(y) = \gamma\,\tilde p_A(y) + (1-\gamma)\,\tilde p_{A^c}(y),
$$
where $\tilde p_A$ and $\tilde p_{A^c}$ are the generator's outputs averaged over $A$ and over its
complement, under the conditional retriever distributions. And $p_{\mathrm{oracle}} = \tilde p_A$.
Hence
$$
p_{\mathrm{RAG}} - p_{\mathrm{oracle}} = (1-\gamma)\big(\tilde p_{A^c} - \tilde p_A\big),
$$
so
$$
\norm{p_{\mathrm{RAG}}-p_{\mathrm{oracle}}}_{\mathrm{TV}}
= (1-\gamma)\,\norm{\tilde p_{A^c}-\tilde p_A}_{\mathrm{TV}} \le 1-\gamma ,
$$
since total variation distance between probability measures is at most $1$.
</Proof>

<Proposition id="error-propagation-tight" title="The bound is attained">
There are instances with equality. Take a binary answer space $\{y_0,y_1\}$, a generator that outputs
$y_1$ with probability $1$ given any $z\in Z^\star$ and $y_0$ with probability $1$ given any
$z\notin Z^\star$. Then $\norm{\tilde p_{A^c}-\tilde p_A}_{\mathrm{TV}}=1$ and the bound holds with
equality.
</Proposition>

<Proof>
Direct substitution into the decomposition above.
</Proof>

<KeyIdea>
Read the bound as: **retrieval error is a hard ceiling, not a soft degradation.** A retriever that
fails $10\%$ of the time permits the output distribution to be wrong on $10\%$ of the probability
mass, and the tightness proposition shows a generator can realise the whole of that error.

There is no averaging-out. One might hope that the $k-1$ irrelevant documents in the context act as
neutral noise the model ignores; the evidence is against that. Shi et al. show that irrelevant
context measurably degrades reasoning accuracy, and Liu et al. show that relevant context placed in
the middle of a long prompt is used substantially less than context at either end. So the failure
modes compound rather than cancel: missing documents cap the achievable quality, and *extra*
documents actively hurt.

Combined with the <TopicRef id="rag/vector-search-and-ann">multiplicative recall</TopicRef> of the
pipeline, the arithmetic is unforgiving. A chunker that splits the answer across boundaries
($\gamma_1 = 0.95$), an ANN index at $\gamma_2 = 0.9$, and a top-$k$ truncation at $\gamma_3 = 0.95$
give $\gamma \approx 0.81$ — so nearly a fifth of the output distribution is unconstrained by the
evidence, before the generator makes a single mistake of its own.
</KeyIdea>

## Reranking

<Proposition id="reranking-gain" title="What reranking can and cannot do">
Let the first stage return a candidate set $C$ with $\P(Z^\star\cap C\ne\emptyset) = \gamma_1$, and let
a reranker select the top $k'<|C|$ from $C$. Then the post-rerank recall satisfies
$$
\gamma_{\text{rerank}} = \gamma_1\cdot\P\big(\text{relevant document in top }k' \mid \text{it is in } C\big) \le \gamma_1 .
$$
Reranking can only improve the **precision** of the final set, never the recall of the pipeline.
</Proposition>

<Proof>
The reranker's output is a subset of $C$, so the relevant document is in the output only if it was in
$C$. Conditioning on that event gives the product, and the second factor is at most $1$.
</Proof>

<Note title="Why rerankers are used anyway">
Precision matters for two reasons the recall bound does not capture. Context length is finite and
expensive, so a reranker lets a large $|C|$ (high $\gamma_1$) be reduced to a small $k'$ that fits.
And by the distraction results above, *removing* irrelevant documents improves generation quality
directly. So the right design is: retrieve broadly with a cheap bi-encoder to maximise $\gamma_1$,
then rerank aggressively with an expensive cross-encoder to maximise precision. The cross-encoder can
afford full query–document attention because it scores only $|C|$ pairs rather than the whole corpus;
that asymmetry is the entire architectural reason for the two stages.
</Note>

## The variant landscape

<Claim kind="heuristic" id="rag-variants" title="RAG variants as choices about the latent structure">
The named RAG variants are all interventions on the posterior $p_\eta(z\mid x)$ or on the latent
space $\Zcal$, within the same probabilistic model.
</Claim>

| Variant | What it changes | In the model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Naive RAG | — | one-shot $p_\eta(z\mid x)$, top-$k$, concatenate |
| Hybrid retrieval | scoring function | $p_\eta \propto \exp(\lambda s_{\text{dense}} + (1-\lambda)s_{\text{BM25}})$ |
| Reranking | truncation of the support | two-stage construction of $p_\eta$ |
| Query expansion / HyDE | the conditioning variable | replace $x$ by $\tilde x = h(x)$ closer to document space |
| Corrective RAG | conditional re-retrieval | evaluate sufficiency, re-draw $z$ if inadequate |
| Agentic / iterative RAG | sequential latent | $z_1,\dots,z_m$ drawn adaptively, a latent *sequence* not a latent variable |
| Graph RAG | the latent space itself | $\Zcal$ becomes a graph; retrieval returns a subgraph, so multi-hop paths are single latents |
| Fusion-in-Decoder | where mixing happens | attention over separately encoded documents, not an explicit sum |

<NotSaid>
Two honest caveats about this table.

First, the fact that all of these fit one framework does not mean they are equally good or that the
framework predicts which will win. The probabilistic view organises the design space; it does not
supply a theory of retrieval quality, and the comparative evidence between variants is empirical and
benchmark-dependent.

Second, the iterative and agentic variants strictly leave the single-latent model: with $m$ adaptive
retrieval rounds the object is a latent *trajectory*, the marginalisation is over sequences, and the
error analysis above does not apply directly — each round's failure probability compounds, and there
is no clean analogue of the total-variation bound. That is a genuine gap: the systems being built are
ahead of the analysis, and the error propagation for multi-round retrieval has not, to this author's
knowledge, been characterised.
</NotSaid>
