An embedding model is a map $f:\Xcal\to\R^d$ from text (or images, or anything) to vectors. Retrieval
ranks documents by a similarity $s(f(q), f(z))$ between a query embedding and a document embedding.
This page is about the geometry that ranking lives in.

## Three similarities and how they relate

<Proposition id="similarity-equivalence" title="Cosine, dot product and Euclidean distance agree on normalised vectors">
For $u,v$ with $\norm u_2 = \norm v_2 = 1$,
$$
\norm{u-v}_2^2 = 2 - 2\ip uv,
\qquad
\cos(u,v) = \ip uv .
$$
Hence ranking by largest dot product, largest cosine, or smallest Euclidean distance gives the
**identical ordering** on the unit sphere.
</Proposition>

<Proof>
$\norm{u-v}_2^2 = \norm u^2 - 2\ip uv + \norm v^2 = 2-2\ip uv$. And
$\cos(u,v) = \ip uv/(\norm u\norm v) = \ip uv$. The first identity is a strictly decreasing function
of $\ip uv$, so the orderings by $-\norm{u-v}_2$ and by $\ip uv$ coincide.
</Proof>

<Proposition id="norm-matters" title="Without normalisation the norm dominates the ranking">
For general $u,v$,
$$
\ip uv = \norm u_2\norm v_2\cos(u,v),
\qquad
\norm{u-v}_2^2 = \norm u_2^2 + \norm v_2^2 - 2\norm u_2\norm v_2\cos(u,v).
$$
So dot-product ranking of documents $v$ against a fixed query $u$ orders by
$\norm v_2\cos(u,v)$, which a document can increase by growing its norm at fixed direction. Euclidean
ranking penalises large $\norm v_2$ instead. The three criteria therefore give three different
orderings.
</Proposition>

<Proof>
Both identities are expansions of the inner product. For the ordering claim, fix a direction
$\hat v$ and consider $v = c\hat v$ for $c>0$: the dot product is $c\,\ip{u}{\hat v}$, increasing in
$c$ whenever $\ip u{\hat v}>0$, while
$\norm{u - c\hat v}_2^2 = \norm u^2 - 2c\ip u{\hat v}+c^2$ is minimised at $c = \ip u{\hat v}$ and
increases thereafter.
</Proof>

<Warning title="Common mistake">
Building an index with a dot-product metric on un-normalised embeddings, and then reasoning about the
results as though they were cosine similarities. Long documents and documents in verbose registers
often receive larger embedding norms, and a dot-product index will surface them preferentially
regardless of relevance. Either normalise at index time — and then all three metrics agree — or make
the norm's role a deliberate modelling choice. Note also that
<TopicRef id="rag/vector-search-and-ann">ANN indexes</TopicRef> often support only one metric, so the
choice is sometimes forced by the infrastructure rather than by the mathematics.
</Warning>

## Near-orthogonality

<Theorem id="cosine-density" title="Distribution of the cosine between random directions">
Let $u,v$ be independent and uniform on $S^{d-1}$, $d\ge3$. Then $t = \ip uv$ has density
$$
\varrho_d(t) = \frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac d2\right)}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)}\big(1-t^2\big)^{\frac{d-3}{2}},
\qquad t\in[-1,1],
$$
with $\E t = 0$, $\Var(t) = 1/d$, and
$$
\P\big(|t|\ge\eps\big) \le 2\,e^{-d\eps^2/2} .
$$
</Theorem>

<Proof>
By rotational invariance condition on $v = e_1$, so $t = u_1$. Realise $u = g/\norm g_2$ with
$g\sim\Ncal(0,I_d)$. The density of $u_1$ is obtained from the surface measure of the sphere: the set
$\{u\in S^{d-1} : u_1 \in [t,t+dt]\}$ is a band whose $(d-2)$-dimensional cross-section is a sphere of
radius $\sqrt{1-t^2}$, giving surface measure proportional to
$(1-t^2)^{(d-2)/2}\cdot(1-t^2)^{-1/2}dt = (1-t^2)^{(d-3)/2}dt$ (the second factor from the
arc-length element). Normalising gives the stated constant, using
$\int_{-1}^1(1-t^2)^{(d-3)/2}dt = \sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(\frac{d-1}2)/\Gamma(\frac d2)$.

Moments: $\E u_1 = 0$ by the symmetry $u\mapsto -u$, and $\sum_i\E u_i^2 = 1$ with all coordinates
identically distributed gives $\E u_1^2 = 1/d$.

The tail bound follows from $(1-t^2)^{(d-3)/2}\le e^{-(d-3)t^2/2}$ together with the constant bound
$\Gamma(d/2)/\Gamma((d-1)/2)\le\sqrt{d/2}$, and integrating the resulting Gaussian tail; the stated
form absorbs the small-$d$ corrections into the constants. (A cleaner route for the tail is the
argument in the <TopicRef id="high-dimensional-probability/concentration-of-the-norm">near-orthogonality
proposition</TopicRef>.)
</Proof>

<ExperimentRef id="embedding-geometry" />

<KeyIdea>
Two independent directions in $\R^{768}$ have cosine within about $0.036 = 1/\sqrt{768}$ of zero.
So a "background" similarity of $0.04$ is what noise looks like, and a genuine match at $0.3$ stands
out by a factor of eight. **This is what makes dense retrieval possible at all**: high dimension
supplies an enormous number of nearly-orthogonal directions, so distinct meanings can be assigned
distinct directions without crowding.

Quantitatively, by the tail bound plus a union bound, one can place $N$ directions with all pairwise
cosines below $\eps$ as long as $N^2e^{-d\eps^2/2}<1$, i.e. $N < e^{d\eps^2/4}$ — **exponentially
many** in the dimension. At $d = 768$ and $\eps = 0.2$ that is $e^{7.7}\approx 2200$ strictly
$\eps$-separated directions by this crude bound, and far more with a sharper packing argument; the
exact statement is the <TopicRef id="high-dimensional-probability/nets-covering-and-packing">packing
number</TopicRef> of the sphere.
</KeyIdea>

## The maximum-of-N problem

Retrieval is not a pairwise question. The relevant document must beat **all** $N$ distractors, so the
quantity that matters is the maximum of $N$ similarities.

<Theorem id="max-of-n" title="The maximum of N distractor similarities">
Let $z_1,\dots,z_N$ be independent uniform directions and $q$ a fixed unit vector. Then
$$
\E\left[\max_{j\le N}\ip{q}{z_j}\right] \le \sqrt{\frac{2\log N}{d}} ,
$$
and with probability at least $1-\delta$,
$$
\max_{j\le N}\ip q{z_j} \le \sqrt{\frac{2\log(N/\delta)}{d}} .
$$
</Theorem>

<Proof>
Each $\ip q{z_j}$ is sub-Gaussian with parameter $O(1/\sqrt d)$ by the previous theorem's tail bound.
Apply <TopicRef id="learning-theory/vc-dimension-and-rademacher">Massart's maximal inequality</TopicRef>
— or directly, the MGF argument: for $\lambda>0$,
$$
\exp\left(\lambda\,\E\max_j\ip q{z_j}\right) \le \E\exp\left(\lambda\max_j\ip q{z_j}\right)
\le \sum_{j=1}^N\E e^{\lambda\ip q{z_j}} \le Ne^{\lambda^2/(2d)} ,
$$
using Jensen for the first step and the sub-Gaussian MGF bound with parameter $1/\sqrt d$ for the
last. Taking logarithms and dividing,
$$
\E\max_j\ip q{z_j} \le \frac{\log N}{\lambda}+\frac{\lambda}{2d},
$$
minimised at $\lambda = \sqrt{2d\log N}$ with value $\sqrt{2\log N/d}$. The high-probability version
is the tail bound plus a union bound over the $N$ distractors.
</Proof>

<Corollary id="retrieval-condition" title="A sufficient condition for correct top-1 retrieval">
Suppose the relevant document $z^\star$ satisfies $\ip{q}{z^\star} = \alpha$ and the $N$ distractors
are independent uniform directions. Then top-1 retrieval succeeds with probability at least
$1-\delta$ provided
$$
\alpha > \sqrt{\frac{2\log(N/\delta)}{d}} .
$$
</Corollary>

<Proof>
On the event of the previous theorem's high-probability bound, every distractor score is below
$\sqrt{2\log(N/\delta)/d}$, which is below $\alpha$ by hypothesis. So $z^\star$ ranks first.
</Proof>

<KeyIdea>
Put numbers in. At $d = 768$ and $\delta = 0.05$:

| Corpus size $N$ | Required alignment $\alpha$ |
| --- | --- |
| $10^3$ | $0.16$ |
| $10^6$ | $0.20$ |
| $10^9$ | $0.24$ |
| $10^{12}$ | $0.27$ |

The threshold grows like $\sqrt{\log N}$ — **slowly, but it grows**, and there is no corpus size at
which it stops. Tripling the required alignment is not possible by scaling the index; it requires a
better embedding model or a re-ranking stage. Conversely, raising $d$ helps as $1/\sqrt d$, which is
the mathematical case for larger embedding dimensions.
</KeyIdea>

<Claim kind="empirical" id="anisotropy" title="Real embeddings are anisotropic">
Learned embeddings do not occupy the sphere uniformly. Ethayarajh measured average cosine
similarities between random *unrelated* sentences in contextual models and found values of $0.3$–$0.6$
in some layers — far above the $1/\sqrt d \approx 0.04$ the isotropic model predicts. The embeddings
lie in a narrow cone.
</Claim>

<NotSaid>
Every theorem on this page assumes the distractors are **independent uniform directions**. Anisotropy
breaks that assumption in the direction that makes retrieval harder:

- the baseline similarity is inflated, so the *contrast* between a match and a non-match shrinks;
- the distractors are correlated, so the maximum-of-$N$ bound — which is driven by the effective number of independent directions — is not the right calculation, and the effective $N$ can behave like a much larger number of truly independent draws in the cone's few informative directions;
- near-duplicate documents, which real corpora are full of, are the worst case: they are highly correlated with the true match and the maximum-of-$N$ analysis says nothing about them.

The standard remedies are all attempts to restore approximate isotropy: mean-centring, whitening
(BERT-flow and successors), and contrastive training with in-batch negatives, which explicitly
penalises high similarity between unrelated pairs and is why models like Sentence-BERT and DPR behave
far better than raw pretrained encoders.

Read this page's thresholds as a **best case**: the alignment actually required on real corpora is
higher, and how much higher is an empirical question about the specific embedding model, not something
the geometry determines.
</NotSaid>
