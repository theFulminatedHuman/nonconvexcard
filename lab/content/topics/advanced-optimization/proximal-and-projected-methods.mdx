Unconstrained gradient descent needs one modification to handle constraints and non-smooth
regularisers, and the modification is the same in every case: replace "take a gradient step" by
"take a gradient step, then *do something* that respects the structure". This page collects the four
standard choices of "something".

## Projection

<Definition id="projection-def" title="Euclidean projection">
For a closed convex $\mathcal{C}\subseteq\R^d$,
$\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(x) = \argmin_{z\in\mathcal{C}}\norm{x-z}_2$. The minimiser exists and is unique
by strong convexity of the objective on the closed convex feasible set.
</Definition>

<Lemma id="projection-nonexpansive" title="Projection is non-expansive">
For all $x,y\in\R^d$ and all $z\in\mathcal{C}$,
$$
\text{(i)}\quad \ip{x - \Pi_\mathcal{C}(x)}{z - \Pi_\mathcal{C}(x)} \le 0,
\qquad
\text{(ii)}\quad \norm{\Pi_\mathcal{C}(x)-\Pi_\mathcal{C}(y)}_2 \le \norm{x-y}_2 .
$$
</Lemma>

<Proof>
(i) Let $p = \Pi_\mathcal{C}(x)$ and $z\in\mathcal{C}$. For $\theta\in(0,1]$ the point
$p + \theta(z-p)$ is in $\mathcal{C}$ by convexity, so by optimality of $p$,
$$
\norm{x - p}_2^2 \le \norm{x - p - \theta(z-p)}_2^2
= \norm{x-p}_2^2 - 2\theta\ip{x-p}{z-p} + \theta^2\norm{z-p}_2^2 .
$$
Cancelling and dividing by $\theta>0$ gives $2\ip{x-p}{z-p}\le\theta\norm{z-p}_2^2$; letting
$\theta\downarrow0$ gives (i).

(ii) Write $p = \Pi_\mathcal{C}(x)$, $q = \Pi_\mathcal{C}(y)$. Apply (i) at $x$ with $z = q$ and at
$y$ with $z = p$:
$$
\ip{x-p}{q-p}\le0, \qquad \ip{y-q}{p-q}\le0 .
$$
Adding the two (after negating the second) gives $\ip{(x-y)-(p-q)}{p-q}\ge0$, i.e.
$\norm{p-q}_2^2 \le \ip{x-y}{p-q} \le \norm{x-y}_2\norm{p-q}_2$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. Dividing by
$\norm{p-q}_2$ (or noting the claim is trivial when it vanishes) completes the proof.
</Proof>

<Theorem id="projected-gd-rate" title="Convergence of projected gradient descent">
Let $f$ be convex and $L$-smooth on a closed convex $\mathcal{C}$, with minimiser $x^\star$ over
$\mathcal{C}$. The iteration
$$
x_{t+1} = \Pi_\mathcal{C}\big(x_t - \tfrac1L\nabla f(x_t)\big)
$$
satisfies $f(x_t) - f(x^\star) \le \dfrac{L\norm{x_0-x^\star}_2^2}{2t}$, the same rate as in the
unconstrained case.
</Theorem>

<Proof>
Let $y_{t+1} = x_t - \tfrac1L\nabla f(x_t)$ be the unprojected point, so $x_{t+1} = \Pi_\mathcal{C}(y_{t+1})$.
Since $x^\star\in\mathcal{C}$, part (ii) of the lemma with the fact that $\Pi_\mathcal{C}(x^\star) = x^\star$
gives
$$
\norm{x_{t+1}-x^\star}_2 = \norm{\Pi_\mathcal{C}(y_{t+1}) - \Pi_\mathcal{C}(x^\star)}_2 \le \norm{y_{t+1}-x^\star}_2 .
$$
So the projection can only help: every step of the unconstrained analysis that bounded
$\norm{x_{t+1}-x^\star}_2^2$ from above by an expression in $\norm{x_t-x^\star}_2^2$ now bounds
$\norm{y_{t+1}-x^\star}_2^2$ by the same expression, and the projected iterate is no further away.
Running the <TopicRef id="optimization/gradient-descent-convergence">unconstrained proof</TopicRef>
with this substitution, the telescoping argument goes through unchanged, with one adjustment: the
convexity step needs $\ip{\nabla f(x_t)}{x_t-x^\star}\ge f(x_t)-f(x^\star)$, which holds because
both points are in $\mathcal{C}$ and $f$ is convex there, and the descent step needs the descent
lemma applied at $y_{t+1}$ followed by the non-expansiveness above.
</Proof>

<Note title="When projection is cheap">
Projected gradient descent is only useful when $\Pi_\mathcal{C}$ is cheap. It is, for: a box
($\Pi(x)_i = \operatorname{clip}(x_i)$), a Euclidean ball ($x\mapsto x\min(1,r/\norm x_2)$), the
probability simplex ($O(d\log d)$ by sorting), a subspace (a linear solve), the PSD cone (eigenvalue
clipping). It is not cheap for a general polytope, which is the motivation for Frank–Wolfe below.
</Note>

## The proximal operator

<Definition id="prox-def" title="Proximal operator">
For a convex, closed, proper $h:\R^d\to\R\cup\{+\infty\}$ and $\eta>0$,
$$
\prox_{\eta h}(v) = \argmin_{z}\left\{h(z) + \frac{1}{2\eta}\norm{z-v}_2^2\right\}.
$$
The minimiser is unique because the objective is strongly convex.
</Definition>

Taking $h = \iota_\mathcal{C}$, the indicator function of a convex set ($0$ on $\mathcal{C}$,
$+\infty$ off it), recovers $\prox_{\eta\iota_\mathcal{C}} = \Pi_\mathcal{C}$. So projection is a
special case of the proximal operator, and the theory below subsumes the theory above.

<Proposition id="prox-soft-threshold" title="Prox of the l1 norm is soft thresholding">
For $h(z) = \norm{z}_1$,
$$
\big[\prox_{\eta h}(v)\big]_i = \sign(v_i)\max\big(|v_i| - \eta,\ 0\big) =: S_\eta(v_i).
$$
</Proposition>

<Proof>
The objective $\sum_i\big(|z_i| + \tfrac{1}{2\eta}(z_i-v_i)^2\big)$ separates across coordinates, so
solve the scalar problem $\min_z |z| + \tfrac{1}{2\eta}(z-v)^2$.

For $z>0$ the derivative is $1 + (z-v)/\eta$, vanishing at $z = v - \eta$, which is positive exactly
when $v>\eta$. For $z<0$ the derivative is $-1+(z-v)/\eta$, vanishing at $z = v+\eta$, negative
exactly when $v<-\eta$. For $|v|\le\eta$ neither stationary point is admissible, and $0$ is optimal:
the subdifferential of $|z|$ at $0$ is $[-1,1]$, and the optimality condition
$0 \in \partial|z| + (z-v)/\eta$ at $z=0$ reads $v/\eta \in[-1,1]$, i.e. $|v|\le\eta$. Collecting the
three cases gives $S_\eta$.
</Proof>

<KeyIdea>
Soft thresholding is why the <TopicRef id="high-dimensional-statistics/sparse-recovery-and-the-lasso">lasso</TopicRef>
produces exactly-zero coefficients rather than merely small ones. A gradient step followed by soft
thresholding sets to zero every coordinate whose gradient signal is below $\eta$ — the sparsity is
created by the prox operator, in closed form, at no computational cost. Ridge regression, whose prox
is the shrinkage $v/(1+2\eta)$, never produces an exact zero.
</KeyIdea>

<Theorem id="ista-rate" title="Convergence of proximal gradient descent">
Let $F = f + h$ with $f$ convex and $L$-smooth and $h$ convex closed proper. The iteration
$$
x_{t+1} = \prox_{h/L}\big(x_t - \tfrac1L\nabla f(x_t)\big)
$$
satisfies $F(x_t)-F^\star \le \dfrac{L\norm{x_0-x^\star}_2^2}{2t}$. The accelerated variant (FISTA)
attains $O(1/t^2)$.
</Theorem>

Quoted from Beck–Teboulle. The structure of the proof is the projected-gradient proof with the
non-expansiveness of $\Pi_\mathcal{C}$ replaced by the firm non-expansiveness of $\prox$; the
references give it in full.

<Algorithm title="ISTA — proximal gradient for the lasso">
<ol>
<li><strong>Input.</strong> Design $X\in\R^{n\times p}$, response $y$, regularisation $\lambda$, step $\eta = 1/L$ with $L = \norm{X^\top X}/n$.</li>
<li><strong>Repeat</strong> for $t = 0,1,2,\dots$:</li>
<li>  gradient step $v_t = \beta_t - \dfrac{\eta}{n} X^\top(X\beta_t - y)$;</li>
<li>  soft threshold $\beta_{t+1} = S_{\eta\lambda}(v_t)$, coordinatewise;</li>
<li><strong>Until</strong> $\norm{\beta_{t+1}-\beta_t}_2 \le \text{tol}$.</li>
</ol>
</Algorithm>

## Frank–Wolfe

When projection is expensive but **linear** minimisation over $\mathcal{C}$ is cheap — as for the
$\ell^1$ ball, the nuclear-norm ball, or a combinatorial polytope — the conditional gradient method
applies.

<Theorem id="frank-wolfe-rate" title="Frank–Wolfe convergence">
Let $f$ be convex and $L$-smooth on a compact convex $\mathcal{C}$ of diameter $D$. The iteration
$$
s_t = \argmin_{s\in\mathcal{C}}\ip{\nabla f(x_t)}{s},
\qquad
x_{t+1} = (1-\gamma_t)x_t + \gamma_ts_t, \quad \gamma_t = \frac{2}{t+2},
$$
satisfies $f(x_t) - f^\star \le \dfrac{2LD^2}{t+2}$.
</Theorem>

<Proof>
Let $\Delta_t = f(x_t)-f^\star$ and note $x_{t+1}-x_t = \gamma_t(s_t-x_t)$, of norm at most
$\gamma_tD$. By the descent lemma,
$$
f(x_{t+1}) \le f(x_t) + \gamma_t\ip{\nabla f(x_t)}{s_t-x_t} + \frac{L\gamma_t^2D^2}{2}.
$$
By the choice of $s_t$ and then convexity,
$$
\ip{\nabla f(x_t)}{s_t-x_t} \le \ip{\nabla f(x_t)}{x^\star - x_t} \le f^\star - f(x_t) = -\Delta_t .
$$
Hence $\Delta_{t+1}\le(1-\gamma_t)\Delta_t + \tfrac{L\gamma_t^2D^2}{2}$.

Now induct on the claim $\Delta_t \le \frac{2LD^2}{t+2}$. It holds at $t=0$ because
$\Delta_0 \le \ip{\nabla f(x_0)}{x_0 - x^\star} \le LD^2$ (by smoothness and the diameter bound), and
$LD^2 \le 2LD^2/2$. Assuming it at $t$, with $\gamma_t = 2/(t+2)$,
$$
\Delta_{t+1}\le\left(1-\frac{2}{t+2}\right)\frac{2LD^2}{t+2} + \frac{L D^2}{2}\cdot\frac{4}{(t+2)^2}
= \frac{2LD^2}{t+2}\cdot\frac{t}{t+2} + \frac{2LD^2}{(t+2)^2}
= \frac{2LD^2(t+1)}{(t+2)^2}.
$$
Finally $\frac{t+1}{(t+2)^2} \le \frac{1}{t+3}$, since $(t+1)(t+3) = t^2+4t+3 \le (t+2)^2 = t^2+4t+4$.
So $\Delta_{t+1}\le\frac{2LD^2}{t+3}$, completing the induction.
</Proof>

<Note title="Why Frank–Wolfe is used in practice">
Two properties. The iterate is a convex combination of at most $t+1$ vertices of $\mathcal{C}$, so it
is automatically **sparse** when the vertices are (e.g. low-rank for the nuclear-norm ball). And the
quantity $\ip{\nabla f(x_t)}{x_t - s_t}$ computed at each step is a valid **duality gap certificate**,
upper bounding $\Delta_t$ — a stopping criterion for free. The price is the $O(1/t)$ rate, which
cannot be improved to $O(1/t^2)$ in general for projection-free methods on arbitrary compact sets.
</Note>

## Mirror descent

<Proposition id="mirror-descent-note" title="Mirror descent and geometry">
Let $\Phi$ be $1$-strongly convex with respect to a norm $\norm{\cdot}$ on $\mathcal{C}$, and define
the Bregman divergence $D_\Phi(x,y) = \Phi(x)-\Phi(y)-\ip{\nabla\Phi(y)}{x-y}$. The iteration
$$
x_{t+1} = \argmin_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\left\{\eta\ip{\nabla f(x_t)}{x} + D_\Phi(x,x_t)\right\}
$$
satisfies, for convex $f$ with $\norm{\nabla f}_* \le G$ in the dual norm,
$$
f(\bar x_T) - f^\star \le \frac{D_\Phi(x^\star,x_0)}{\eta T} + \frac{\eta G^2}{2},
$$
so with optimal $\eta$ the rate is $G\sqrt{2D_\Phi(x^\star,x_0)/T}$.
</Proposition>

The proof is the subgradient-method proof with $\tfrac12\norm{\cdot}_2^2$ replaced by $D_\Phi$
throughout; it is in Bubeck's survey. The content is the choice of $\Phi$:

| $\mathcal{C}$ | $\Phi$ | Norm | Rate constant |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\R^d$ | $\tfrac12\norm{x}_2^2$ | $\ell^2$ | $\norm{x^\star}_2\norm{\nabla f}_2$ |
| simplex $\Delta_d$ | $\sum_ix_i\log x_i$ | $\ell^1$ | $\sqrt{\log d}\,\norm{\nabla f}_\infty$ |

On the simplex the entropic choice gives a $\sqrt{\log d}$ dependence on dimension where the
Euclidean choice would give $\sqrt d$. That is a genuinely large gain for large $d$, and it is
obtained by measuring the gradient in $\ell^\infty$ rather than $\ell^2$ — the geometry of the problem,
not the algorithm, is what changed.

<KeyIdea>
Gradient descent is not "the" first-order method; it is the first-order method for the Euclidean
geometry. The <TopicRef id="foundations/matrix-calculus">gradient</TopicRef> is a representation of
the derivative that depends on an inner product, and mirror descent makes that dependence a design
choice. Adaptive methods such as Adam can be read the same way, as descent in a metric that changes
with the iterates.
</KeyIdea>
