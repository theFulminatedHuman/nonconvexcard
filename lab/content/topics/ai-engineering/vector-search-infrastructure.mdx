<Intuition>
A vector index is an approximation with a dial on it. Turn the dial one way and queries are fast
and sometimes miss the right document; turn it the other and they are slow and thorough. Almost
every operational question about a vector database is really a question about where that dial
should sit, and the answer comes from what a miss costs downstream.
</Intuition>

<Definition id="index-tradeoffs" title="What each index type costs">
| | Build | Memory | Query | Recall | Updates |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Flat** (exhaustive) | none | vectors only | $O(N)$ | 1.0 exactly | trivial |
| **IVF** (inverted file) | $k$-means, minutes | vectors + centroids | $O(N/k \cdot n_{\text{probe}})$ | tunable via `nprobe` | cheap inserts, drifts from centroids |
| **HNSW** (graph) | slow, $O(N \log N)$ | vectors + graph, ~1.5× | $O(\log N)$ | tunable via `ef_search` | inserts fine, deletes are tombstones |

Flat is correct and is the right answer far more often than people expect: at a hundred thousand
vectors of 768 dimensions, exhaustive search is a few hundred megabytes and single-digit
milliseconds. **Do not deploy an approximate index until exhaustive search is genuinely too slow**,
because the approximate one costs you a recall number you now have to monitor.
</Definition>

<Claim kind="proposition" id="recall-ceiling" title="Retrieval recall caps end-to-end quality">
Let $R$ be the probability that the document needed to answer is in the retrieved set, and let $A$
be the probability the model answers correctly given it is present. Assuming the model cannot
answer correctly without it, end-to-end accuracy is
$$
\Pr[\text{correct}] \;=\; R \cdot A \;\le\; R .
$$
</Claim>

<Proof>
Condition on whether the document was retrieved. If it was not — probability $1-R$ — the answer is
incorrect by assumption, contributing nothing. If it was, the model is correct with probability
$A$. The product is the display, bounded above by $R$.
</Proof>

<KeyIdea>
Recall is a ceiling, and it multiplies with every other stage exactly as
<TopicRef id="ai-systems/caching" /> and the pipeline-recall argument describe. Tuning `ef_search`
from 40 to 200 to move recall from 0.92 to 0.98 raises the ceiling by six points — frequently more
than a prompt change achieves, and far more measurable.
</KeyIdea>

<Definition id="filter-strategies" title="Three ways to combine filters with vector search">
Almost every real query is "nearest neighbours **where** tenant = X and date > Y".

| Strategy | How | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| **post-filter** | retrieve top-$k$, then drop non-matching | the filter is selective — you asked for 10 and got 2 |
| **pre-filter** | restrict the candidate set, then search | the set is large; you have lost the index |
| **filtered traversal** | apply the predicate during graph/list traversal | needs index support, but is the correct answer |

Post-filtering is the default in naive implementations and is the source of the "why did I only get
three results?" bug. With a filter matching 1% of the corpus, a top-100 retrieval yields about one
result.
</Definition>

<Warning title="Tenant isolation is a filter you cannot get wrong">
Multi-tenant indexes filtered by `tenant_id` are one bug away from returning another customer's
documents. Prefer a separate namespace or index per tenant where the tenancy is coarse: it makes
cross-tenant leakage structurally impossible rather than dependent on a predicate being applied
correctly on every path.
</Warning>

<Claim kind="heuristic" id="freshness" title="Two-tier indexing for freshness">
Graph indexes are much cheaper to build in bulk than to update one vector at a time, and deletes
leave tombstones that degrade the graph. The standard resolution:

```
query ──┬──► hot index   (flat, last few hours, rebuilt every few minutes)
        └──► cold index  (HNSW, everything, rebuilt nightly)
              └── results merged and re-ranked by true distance
```

New documents are searchable within minutes at flat-search cost, because the hot index is small.
The cold index is rebuilt offline, which also removes accumulated tombstones and re-centres IVF
centroids that have drifted.
</Claim>

<NotSaid>
Dense retrieval alone under-performs on exact identifiers — part numbers, error codes, names — where
lexical matching is strictly better. Production retrieval is usually hybrid, combining BM25 and
dense scores, and the combination weight is another dial that needs the same recall measurement as
`ef_search`.
</NotSaid>
