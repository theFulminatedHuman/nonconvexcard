## Attention

Let $X\in\R^{n\times d}$ hold $n$ token representations as rows.

<Definition id="attention-def" title="Scaled dot-product attention">
$$
Q = XW_Q,\quad K = XW_K,\quad V = XW_V,
\qquad W_Q,W_K\in\R^{d\times d_k},\ W_V\in\R^{d\times d_v},
$$
$$
\operatorname{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \underbrace{\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)}_{A\ \in\ \R^{n\times n}}V ,
$$
with the softmax applied **row-wise**. $A$ is row-stochastic: $A_{ij}\ge0$ and
$\sum_jA_{ij}=1$, so row $i$ of the output is a convex combination of the value vectors.
</Definition>

<Intuition>
Each token emits a **query** describing what it is looking for and a **key** describing what it
offers. The logit $\ip{q_i}{k_j}$ scores how well token $j$ answers token $i$'s query, the softmax
turns the scores into a probability distribution over positions, and the output is the corresponding
average of value vectors. The operation is *content-based lookup with soft weights*, and it is
differentiable precisely because the lookup is soft.
</Intuition>

<Proposition id="sqrt-dk-scaling" title="Why the scaling is 1/sqrt(d_k)">
Suppose the entries of $q_i$ and $k_j$ are independent with mean $0$ and variance $\sigma^2$, as at
standard initialisation. Then
$$
\E\ip{q_i}{k_j} = 0, \qquad \Var\big(\ip{q_i}{k_j}\big) = d_k\sigma^4 ,
$$
so $\ip{q_i}{k_j}$ has standard deviation $\sigma^2\sqrt{d_k}$, growing with $d_k$. Dividing by
$\sqrt{d_k}$ makes the standard deviation $\sigma^2$, independent of the head dimension.
</Proposition>

<Proof>
$\ip{q}{k} = \sum_{m=1}^{d_k}q_mk_m$ with all $2d_k$ factors independent and centred. The mean is
$\sum_m\E q_m\E k_m = 0$. For the variance, the summands $q_mk_m$ are independent with
$\E[q_mk_m]=0$ and $\E[(q_mk_m)^2] = \E q_m^2\E k_m^2 = \sigma^4$, so the variance of the sum is
$d_k\sigma^4$.
</Proof>

<Proposition id="softmax-saturation" title="Softmax saturation kills the gradient">
Let $a = \operatorname{softmax}(s)$ for logits $s\in\R^n$. Then
$$
\frac{\partial a}{\partial s} = \diag(a) - aa^\top ,
$$
and for any $v$, $v^\top\big(\diag(a)-aa^\top\big)v = \Var_{j\sim a}(v_j)$. Hence if $a$ concentrates
on one index, the Jacobian is approximately zero.
</Proposition>

<Proof>
The Jacobian is computed in the
<TopicRef id="foundations/matrix-calculus">softmax derivation</TopicRef>. For the quadratic form,
$$
v^\top(\diag(a)-aa^\top)v = \sum_ja_jv_j^2 - \Big(\sum_ja_jv_j\Big)^2
= \E_{j\sim a}[v_j^2] - \big(\E_{j\sim a}[v_j]\big)^2 = \Var_{j\sim a}(v_j).
$$
If $a \to e_{j_0}$ then the distribution is degenerate and its variance is $0$.
</Proof>

<KeyIdea>
Combine the two propositions. Without the $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ factor, logits would have standard deviation
growing like $\sqrt{d_k}$; with $d_k = 64$ and $\sigma^2=1$ that is a spread of $8$ across positions,
which after softmax is near-one-hot. The Jacobian is then a variance of a near-degenerate
distribution, i.e. near zero, and no gradient flows through the attention weights. The scaling is not
cosmetic: it is what keeps the softmax in its informative regime at initialisation.
</KeyIdea>

<Proposition id="attention-permutation" title="Attention is permutation equivariant">
Let $P$ be an $n\times n$ permutation matrix. Then
$$
\operatorname{Attention}\big((PX)W_Q,(PX)W_K,(PX)W_V\big) = P\cdot\operatorname{Attention}(Q,K,V).
$$
Consequently a transformer without positional information computes a function of the input
**multiset**, not the sequence.
</Proposition>

<Proof>
Permuting rows of $X$ permutes rows of $Q,K,V$: $Q\mapsto PQ$ etc. Then
$QK^\top\mapsto PQK^\top P^\top$, and the row-wise softmax commutes with the simultaneous row and
column permutation (each row's entries are permuted, and softmax is permutation equivariant within a
row):
$\operatorname{softmax}(PSP^\top) = P\operatorname{softmax}(S)P^\top$. Finally
$P A P^\top\cdot PV = PAV$ since $P^\top P = I$.
</Proof>

This is why positional encodings are **required**, not an enhancement. The MLP and normalisation
sublayers act position-wise and are therefore also permutation equivariant, so nothing else in the
architecture breaks the symmetry.

## Multi-head attention

$$
\operatorname{MHA}(X) = \operatorname{Concat}\big(\mathrm{head}_1,\dots,\mathrm{head}_h\big)W_O,
\qquad
\mathrm{head}_m = \operatorname{Attention}(XW_Q^m, XW_K^m, XW_V^m),
$$
with $d_k = d_v = d/h$ so that the total parameter count matches a single head of full width.

<Proposition id="multihead-lowrank" title="Multi-head attention is a sum of low-rank attention maps">
Partition $W_O$ into blocks $W_O^m\in\R^{d_v\times d}$. Then
$$
\operatorname{MHA}(X) = \sum_{m=1}^h A^m X\,\underbrace{W_V^mW_O^m}_{\text{rank}\ \le\ d_v = d/h},
$$
where $A^m = \operatorname{softmax}\big(XW_Q^m(W_K^m)^\top X^\top/\sqrt{d_k}\big)$. Each head
contributes an attention pattern $A^m$ composed with a rank-$\le d/h$ value transformation, and
each logit matrix is determined by the rank-$\le d/h$ matrix $W_Q^m(W_K^m)^\top$.
</Proposition>

<Proof>
Concatenation followed by $W_O$ is the same as summing the blockwise products:
$\operatorname{Concat}(\dots)W_O = \sum_m \mathrm{head}_mW_O^m$. Substituting
$\mathrm{head}_m = A^mXW_V^m$ gives the display. The rank bound is
$\rank(W_V^mW_O^m)\le\min(d,d_v,d) = d_v$, and similarly $\rank(W_Q^m(W_K^m)^\top)\le d_k$.
</Proof>

<KeyIdea>
The interesting quantity is not $W_Q$ and $W_K$ separately but their product
$W_{QK}^m = W_Q^m(W_K^m)^\top\in\R^{d\times d}$, of rank at most $d/h$ — and likewise
$W_{VO}^m = W_V^mW_O^m$. A head is fully specified by these two low-rank matrices, which is the
observation underlying the circuit-level analyses in the interpretability literature, and also the
observation that makes <TopicRef id="llm/scaling-and-in-context-learning">LoRA</TopicRef> natural: if
the operative objects are already low rank, a low-rank update is an appropriate parameterisation.

Multiple heads are therefore a *rank allocation* choice: $h$ heads of rank $d/h$ each rather than one
map of rank $d$. The total parameter count is the same; what changes is that each head is forced to
attend according to a single low-dimensional similarity structure, and different heads can specialise.
</KeyIdea>

## Positional information

<Definition id="sinusoidal" title="Sinusoidal positional encoding">
$$
\mathrm{PE}(p)_{2m} = \sin\!\left(\frac{p}{10000^{2m/d}}\right),
\qquad
\mathrm{PE}(p)_{2m+1} = \cos\!\left(\frac{p}{10000^{2m/d}}\right),
$$
added to the token embedding at position $p$. Because
$$
\begin{pmatrix}\sin(\omega(p+\Delta))\\\cos(\omega(p+\Delta))\end{pmatrix}
= \begin{pmatrix}\cos\omega\Delta & \sin\omega\Delta\\ -\sin\omega\Delta & \cos\omega\Delta\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}\sin\omega p\\\cos\omega p\end{pmatrix},
$$
a shift by $\Delta$ acts as a fixed linear map on each frequency pair — so relative position is
linearly available to the model.
</Definition>

<Proposition id="rope-property" title="Rotary embeddings make the logit depend only on relative position">
Let $R_p$ be the block-diagonal rotation acting on coordinate pairs $(2m,2m+1)$ by angle
$\theta_mp$, and define $\tilde q_p = R_pq_p$, $\tilde k_{p'} = R_{p'}k_{p'}$. Then
$$
\ip{\tilde q_p}{\tilde k_{p'}} = \ip{q_p}{R_{p'-p}\,k_{p'}} ,
$$
so the attention logit depends on $p$ and $p'$ only through $p'-p$.
</Proposition>

<Proof>
Rotations are orthogonal, so $R_p^\top = R_p^{-1} = R_{-p}$, and $R_pR_{p'} = R_{p+p'}$ because the
angles add blockwise. Hence
$$
\ip{R_pq}{R_{p'}k} = q^\top R_p^\top R_{p'}k = q^\top R_{p'-p}k .
$$
</Proof>

This is why rotary embeddings (RoPE) are now standard: relative position is built into the inner
product exactly, rather than being linearly recoverable as with additive sinusoids, and the
construction extends to unseen positions.

## Residual stream, normalisation and the MLP block

A pre-norm transformer layer is
$$
X \leftarrow X + \operatorname{MHA}\big(\operatorname{LN}(X)\big),
\qquad
X \leftarrow X + \operatorname{MLP}\big(\operatorname{LN}(X)\big),
$$
with $\operatorname{MLP}(x) = W_2\,\rho(W_1x)$, $W_1\in\R^{4d\times d}$, $W_2\in\R^{d\times4d}$.

The additive form means $X$ is a **residual stream** that every sublayer reads from and writes to,
with Jacobian $I + \partial F/\partial X$ — the same identity-path mechanism as the
<TopicRef id="deep-learning/convolution-recurrence-normalisation">LSTM cell state</TopicRef>, with the
gate fixed at one.

<Claim kind="empirical" id="prenorm-postnorm" title="Pre-norm versus post-norm">
The original transformer used post-norm, $X\leftarrow\operatorname{LN}(X+F(X))$, and required a
learning-rate warmup to train at all beyond a modest depth. Pre-norm trains stably without warmup and
is now universal for large models.
</Claim>

<NotSaid>
Xiong et al. give a partial account: in post-norm the expected gradient norm at initialisation grows
with depth, so the first steps are too large and destroy the initialisation; warmup masks this by
starting small. In pre-norm the gradient norm is roughly depth-independent. Their analysis is at
initialisation and under simplifying assumptions, so it explains the need for warmup rather than
everything about the two variants' behaviour throughout training. The practical choice is settled;
the theory is suggestive rather than complete.
</NotSaid>

<Claim kind="heuristic" id="attention-kernel-view" title="Attention as a kernel smoother">
Write the attention output for query $i$ as
$$
o_i = \sum_j \frac{\exp\big(\ip{q_i}{k_j}/\sqrt{d_k}\big)}{\sum_{j'}\exp\big(\ip{q_i}{k_{j'}}/\sqrt{d_k}\big)}\,v_j
= \frac{\sum_j \kappa(q_i,k_j)v_j}{\sum_j\kappa(q_i,k_j)},
\qquad \kappa(q,k) = e^{\ip qk/\sqrt{d_k}} .
$$
This is exactly a Nadaraya–Watson kernel-weighted average with kernel $\kappa$.
</Claim>

<NotSaid>
The analogy is genuinely useful: it explains why attention is a smoother, why temperature (the
$1/\sqrt{d_k}$) controls bandwidth, and it motivates the linear-attention family, which replaces
$\kappa$ by a factorisable $\ip{\phi(q)}{\phi(k)}$ and thereby reduces the cost from $O(n^2d)$ to
$O(nd^2)$.

But $\kappa(q,k) = e^{\ip qk/\sqrt{d_k}}$ is **not symmetric in the sense a kernel smoother assumes**
— queries and keys come from different projections of the same input, so $\kappa$ is a similarity
between two different representations of tokens, not a distance-based kernel on a single space. It is
positive definite as a function of $(q,k)$ pairs via the exponential of a linear kernel, but the
Nadaraya–Watson theory that gives rates for kernel smoothers assumes a fixed kernel and i.i.d. data,
neither of which holds. Treat the analogy as a source of architectural ideas, not as a route to
guarantees.
</NotSaid>

## Complexity

<Proposition id="attention-complexity" title="Complexity of a transformer layer">
For sequence length $n$, model width $d$, and MLP expansion factor $4$:

| Component | FLOPs | Memory (activations) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $Q,K,V$ projections | $3nd^2$ | $3nd$ |
| Logits $QK^\top$ | $n^2d$ | $n^2$ (per head, $hn^2$ total) |
| $AV$ | $n^2d$ | $nd$ |
| Output projection | $nd^2$ | $nd$ |
| MLP | $8nd^2$ | $4nd$ |
| **Total** | $\Theta(nd^2 + n^2d)$ | $\Theta(nd + hn^2)$ |

Attention dominates the MLP when $n \gtrsim d$.
</Proposition>

<Proof>
Each matrix product $\R^{a\times b}\times\R^{b\times c}$ costs $2abc$ FLOPs. $XW_Q$ is
$(n\times d)(d\times d_k)$ per head, $h$ heads, so $hnd\,d_k = nd^2$ per projection and $3nd^2$ for
three. $QK^\top$ is $(n\times d_k)(d_k\times n)$ per head: $hn^2d_k = n^2d$. $AV$ likewise. The MLP
is two products of size $nd\times4d$: $8nd^2$. Summing gives the total.
</Proof>

<KeyIdea>
The crossover at $n\approx d$ is worth internalising. For a model with $d = 4096$ and context
$n = 2048$, the MLP dominates and attention is not the bottleneck. At $n = 128{,}000$ attention
dominates by a factor of $30$. This is why long-context work is about attention specifically, and why
the $O(n^2)$ *memory* for the attention matrix is often the harder constraint than the FLOPs —
FlashAttention's contribution is to compute exact attention without ever materialising the
$n\times n$ matrix, by tiling and recomputation, reducing memory to $O(n)$ at no accuracy cost.
</KeyIdea>

<Note title="Causal masking">
For autoregressive models the logits are masked: $S_{ij}\leftarrow-\infty$ for $j>i$ before the
softmax, so $A$ is lower triangular. This halves the FLOPs but does not change the asymptotics, and
it is what makes the <TopicRef id="llm/language-modelling-and-cross-entropy">autoregressive
factorisation</TopicRef> valid — each position's output depends only on positions at or before it,
which is exactly the conditional structure the loss assumes.
</Note>
