## What problem does this paper solve?

It reconciles the classical bias–variance picture with the observation that interpolating models
generalise, by showing the two live on different parts of the same curve.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

The double-descent curve itself, and the framing of the **interpolation threshold** as the boundary
between two regimes: the classical regime where capacity is a cost, and the modern regime where extra
capacity buys a *smoother* interpolant.

## What assumptions are made?

That among the many interpolating predictors, the learning procedure selects one of small norm — which
is the implicit-bias hypothesis made explicit. The paper's random-feature experiments use the
minimum-norm solution directly.

## What equations matter?

For random-feature regression with $N$ features and $n$ samples, the risk as a function of $N/n$ has
a pole at $N=n$ and decreases for $N>n$; the mechanism is that the minimum-norm interpolant's norm
$\norm{\hat\beta}_2$ *decreases* as $N$ grows past $n$, because more features give more ways to
interpolate and the cheapest one gets cheaper.

## What theorem or result is important?

The paper is primarily empirical and conceptual; the accompanying theory is for random Fourier
features. The rigorous version for linear models came later (Hastie et al., Bartlett et al.).

## What proof techniques are used?

Explicit computation for random-feature models; otherwise experiment.

## What prior mathematics is required?

Kernels and random features; the pseudoinverse and minimum-norm solutions; the conditioning of random
matrices.

## Limitations

The "smoother interpolant" story is a mechanism sketch. It does not say which norm is minimised for a
neural network, and the answer is not known — see the
<TopicRef id="deep-learning-theory/what-is-proven">implicit bias</TopicRef> discussion.

## Why it mattered

It supplied the vocabulary. "Double descent" and "interpolation regime" are now standard terms, and
the paper redirected attention from *capacity* to *which solution the algorithm selects*, which is
where the open problems now are.
