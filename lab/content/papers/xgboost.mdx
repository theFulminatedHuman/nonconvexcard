## What problem does this paper solve?

Making gradient boosting both more accurate and fast enough for large tabular datasets, which is where
it remains the strongest method.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

The regularised objective at round $t$, expanded to second order in the new tree $h$:
$$
\mathcal{L}^{(t)} \approx \sum_{i=1}^n\left[g_ih(x_i) + \tfrac12 h_ih(x_i)^2\right] + \gamma T + \tfrac12\lambda\sum_{j=1}^Tw_j^2 ,
$$
with $g_i,h_i$ the first and second derivatives of the loss at the current prediction, $T$ the number
of leaves and $w_j$ the leaf values.

## What assumptions are made?

That the second-order expansion is adequate — true for the losses in use (squared, logistic), whose
second derivatives are bounded and well behaved.

## What equations matter?

Because the objective is a sum of independent per-leaf quadratics, the optimal leaf value and the
resulting objective are closed-form:
$$
w_j^\star = -\frac{\sum_{i\in I_j}g_i}{\sum_{i\in I_j}h_i + \lambda},
\qquad
\mathcal{L}^\star = -\frac12\sum_{j=1}^T\frac{\big(\sum_{i\in I_j}g_i\big)^2}{\sum_{i\in I_j}h_i+\lambda} + \gamma T .
$$
The split gain is the difference of this quantity before and after a split, which is what the greedy
search maximises. The $\gamma T$ term means a split with insufficient gain is rejected — pruning is
built into the objective rather than being a post-hoc step.

## What theorem or result is important?

No theorem; the derivation above is the mathematical content. Its relation to
<TopicRef id="machine-learning/trees-and-ensembles">first-order gradient boosting</TopicRef> is
exactly that of Newton's method to gradient descent.

## What proof techniques are used?

Per-leaf minimisation of a quadratic; the algebra is elementary and complete.

## What prior mathematics is required?

Second-order Taylor expansion; minimisation of a one-dimensional quadratic; the functional-gradient
view of boosting.

## Limitations

Second-order expansion requires a twice-differentiable loss. Many hyperparameters with substantial
interactions. And the method inherits boosting's general lack of a generalisation theory —
<TopicRef id="machine-learning/trees-and-ensembles">margin arguments</TopicRef> explain the resistance
to overfitting only partially.

## Why it mattered

It is, with LightGBM and CatBoost, the default method for tabular prediction, and it remains
competitive with or superior to deep learning on that data type — a standing reminder that the
dominance of neural networks is domain-specific.
