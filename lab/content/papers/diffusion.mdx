## What problem does this paper solve?

A generative model with a stable, likelihood-based objective and GAN-level sample quality.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

A fixed **forward** process adding Gaussian noise,
$$
q(x_t\mid x_{t-1}) = \Ncal\big(x_t;\sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,x_{t-1},\ \beta_tI\big),
$$
and a learned **reverse** process $p_\theta(x_{t-1}\mid x_t)$. A key identity makes training cheap: the
forward process has a closed form at any $t$,
$$
q(x_t\mid x_0) = \Ncal\big(x_t;\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0,\ (1-\bar\alpha_t)I\big),
\qquad \bar\alpha_t = \prod_{s\le t}(1-\beta_s),
$$
so $x_t$ can be sampled directly without simulating the chain.

## What assumptions are made?

That $\beta_t$ are small enough for the reverse conditionals to be approximately Gaussian, and that
$\bar\alpha_T\approx0$ so $q(x_T)\approx\Ncal(0,I)$. Both are design choices under the modeller's
control, which is why the training is stable.

## What equations matter?

The variational bound is the same
<TopicRef id="probability/expectation-and-conditioning">ELBO</TopicRef> structure as a VAE with $T$
latent layers. The paper's key simplification: reparameterising the mean prediction as a **noise**
prediction and dropping the weighting terms gives
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{simple}} = \E_{t,x_0,\eps}\Big[\big\|\eps - \eps_\theta\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\eps,\ t\big)\big\|_2^2\Big],
$$
an ordinary squared-loss regression. All the structure is in the forward process; the network just
predicts noise.

## What theorem or result is important?

The equivalence, up to a $t$-dependent weighting, between the variational bound and
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{simple}}$, and the connection to denoising score matching: $\eps_\theta$ is
(up to scale) an estimate of $\nabla_{x_t}\log q(x_t)$, the score.

## What proof techniques are used?

Gaussian conditioning identities to obtain the closed forms; algebraic manipulation of the ELBO's
per-step KL terms, each of which is a KL between two Gaussians and therefore closed-form.

## What prior mathematics is required?

Gaussian marginals and conditionals; KL between Gaussians; the ELBO; Markov chains.

## Limitations

Sampling requires $T$ network evaluations ($T=1000$ originally), orders of magnitude slower than a
GAN's single pass — the motivation for the large subsequent literature on fast samplers. The
likelihoods, while computable as a bound, are not competitive with autoregressive models. And the
"approximately Gaussian reverse conditional" assumption degrades if $\beta_t$ are made large to
shorten the chain.

## Why it mattered

It displaced GANs as the dominant approach to image generation within two years, and its objective is
the basis of every current text-to-image and video generation system. It is also a satisfying case
where the practical breakthrough came from *simplifying* a correct variational bound rather than
replacing it.
