## What problem does this paper solve?

Conditional computation: making parameters cheap. If each input activates only a small fraction of
the network, total capacity can grow without the per-example cost growing.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

A gating function over $E$ experts,
$$
G(x) = \operatorname{softmax}\big(\mathrm{TopK}(xW_g + \text{noise},\ k)\big),
\qquad
y = \sum_{e : G(x)_e>0} G(x)_e\,E_e(x),
$$
where $\mathrm{TopK}$ sets all but the largest $k$ logits to $-\infty$ so the softmax is exactly
$k$-sparse.

## What assumptions are made?

That different inputs benefit from different sub-computations, and that a learned gate can find the
partition. Neither is guaranteed, and the degenerate solution — one expert receives everything — is a
stable equilibrium the gate reaches on its own.

## What equations matter?

The load-balancing auxiliary loss. With $f_e$ the fraction of tokens routed to expert $e$ and $P_e$
the mean gate probability for $e$,
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{aux}} = \lambda\,E\sum_{e=1}^E f_eP_e ,
$$
which is minimised when the routing is uniform. This term is not optional: without it, a rich-get-
richer dynamic concentrates all tokens on a few experts — an expert that receives more tokens trains
faster, so the gate prefers it more, and the process runs away.

## What theorem or result is important?

No theorem. Empirically, up to $137$B parameters in $2017$ with per-token compute of a much smaller
dense model, and improved language-modelling and translation results at matched FLOPs.

## What proof techniques are used?

None; the noise added to the gate logits is a heuristic for exploration, analogous to
$\eps$-greedy.

## What prior mathematics is required?

Softmax and its gradient; the fact that $\mathrm{TopK}$ is non-differentiable in the routing decision,
so the gradient flows only through the selected experts' gate values.

## Limitations

All $EP$ parameters must be in memory, so memory bandwidth replaces FLOPs as the bottleneck. Routing
is discrete and the training signal for unselected experts is absent. Load balancing is a permanent
tax on the objective. Fine-tuning sparse models is harder than dense ones. And the
<TopicRef id="llm/scaling-and-in-context-learning">dense scaling laws</TopicRef> do not transfer —
sparse models need their own fitted laws in $(E,k,P)$.

## Why it mattered

It is the origin of the sparse-expert architecture now used in several frontier models, and it
articulated the decoupling of capacity from compute that is the main structural alternative to simply
building a bigger dense model.
