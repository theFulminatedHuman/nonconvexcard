## What problem does this paper solve?

Training instability in deep networks. The paper's stated diagnosis is **internal covariate shift**:
layer $\ell$'s input distribution changes whenever layers $<\ell$ update, so each layer is chasing a
moving target.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

For a feature $z$ with batch statistics $\mu_B,\sigma_B^2$,
$$
\hat z = \gamma\,\frac{z-\mu_B}{\sqrt{\sigma_B^2+\eps}}+\beta ,
$$
with $\gamma,\beta$ learned. At inference, $\mu_B,\sigma_B^2$ are replaced by running averages, so the
train and test computations differ — a subtlety with real consequences.

## What assumptions are made?

That batches are large enough and i.i.d. enough for $\mu_B,\sigma_B$ to be stable estimates. Both
fail for small batches, for sequences of varying length and for distributed training with small
per-device batches, which is why <TopicRef id="deep-learning/transformers">transformers</TopicRef> use
layer norm instead.

## What equations matter?

The normalisation above, and the gradient through it, which must account for the dependence of
$\mu_B,\sigma_B$ on every element of the batch — so the gradient for example $i$ depends on all other
examples in the batch. That coupling is what makes batch norm a genuinely different operation from a
per-example rescaling.

## What theorem or result is important?

None. The paper reports a $14\times$ reduction in training steps to a given accuracy on ImageNet and
demonstrated robustness to larger learning rates.

## What proof techniques are used?

None.

## What prior mathematics is required?

Backpropagation through a function of batch statistics; the
<TopicRef id="deep-learning/convolution-recurrence-normalisation">scale-invariance</TopicRef>
property of the normalised layer.

## Limitations

The stated mechanism is not supported. Santurkar et al. injected distribution shift after the batch
norm layers and the benefit persisted, which contradicts the covariate-shift account. The train/test
discrepancy causes real bugs, and the batch dependence complicates small-batch and distributed
training.

## Why it mattered

It made much deeper networks trainable and is one of the highest-impact papers in the field. It is
also the clearest case study in deep learning of a correct empirical finding with an incorrect
explanation — worth remembering when reading any mechanism claim in this literature.
