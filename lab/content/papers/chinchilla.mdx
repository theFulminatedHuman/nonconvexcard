## What problem does this paper solve?

Whether the previous compute-optimal prescription was right. It was not, and the error was costing
substantial compute across the field.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

The parametric fit
$$
\hat{\mathcal{L}}(N,D) = E + \frac{A}{N^\alpha} + \frac{B}{D^\beta},
$$
fitted jointly by three independent methods (fixed model sizes with varying tokens; IsoFLOP profiles;
direct parametric fitting), all agreeing.

## What assumptions are made?

That the learning-rate schedule is matched to each run's token budget — precisely the methodological
correction. Under a cosine schedule truncated early, a run's loss is not representative of a properly
scheduled run of that length, which biased the earlier fit.

## What equations matter?

The fitted exponents $\alpha\approx0.34$, $\beta\approx0.28$, giving via the
<TopicRef id="llm/scaling-and-in-context-learning">Lagrange computation</TopicRef>
$$
N^\star\propto C^{0.50},\qquad D^\star\propto C^{0.50},
$$
so $D^\star/N^\star$ is approximately constant at $\approx20$ tokens per parameter.

## What theorem or result is important?

The empirical validation: Chinchilla ($70$B parameters, $1.4$T tokens) outperforms Gopher ($280$B,
$300$B tokens) on essentially every benchmark at the same training compute.

## What proof techniques are used?

Three independent fitting methodologies as a consistency check — good empirical practice, and the
reason the result was believed immediately.

## What prior mathematics is required?

Constrained optimisation of a two-term power law; regression and model-selection basics.

## Limitations

The fit is for a specific architecture family, tokeniser and data distribution. It optimises training
compute only, ignoring inference — for a widely-served model, training well past the Chinchilla point
is rational and is now common practice. And the refitting itself has been questioned on
reproducibility grounds, which is a reminder that the exponents are estimates with uncertainty.

## Why it mattered

It changed how frontier models are built within months of publication, and it is the clearest
demonstration that a scaling-law fit is a piece of *statistics* whose conclusions depend on
methodological care, not a law of nature.
