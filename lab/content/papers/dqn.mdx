## What problem does this paper solve?

The combination of reinforcement learning with deep function approximation, which was known to be
unstable: the "deadly triad" of bootstrapping, off-policy learning and function approximation.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

The deep Q-network $Q_\theta(s,a)$ trained on the Bellman residual
$$
\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \E_{(s,a,r,s')\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\Big(r + \gamma\max_{a'}Q_{\theta^-}(s',a') - Q_\theta(s,a)\Big)^2\right],
$$
where $\mathcal{D}$ is a replay buffer and $\theta^-$ is a periodically-copied frozen parameter set.

## What assumptions are made?

Markovian dynamics (approximated by stacking four frames), a fixed discount $\gamma$, and that the
replay buffer's sample distribution is adequate for the expectation. The last is the weakest link:
the buffer is off-policy and non-stationary.

## What equations matter?

The gradient
$$
\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L} = -2\,\E\Big[\big(r+\gamma\max_{a'}Q_{\theta^-}(s',a') - Q_\theta(s,a)\big)\nabla_\theta Q_\theta(s,a)\Big]
$$
is a **semi-gradient**: it deliberately does not differentiate the target. That is why it is not the
gradient of any objective, and why standard
<TopicRef id="stochastic-optimization/sgd-convergence">SGD convergence theory</TopicRef> does not
apply. The frozen $\theta^-$ makes the target stationary within an update window, which is precisely
what restores something resembling a supervised regression problem.

## What theorem or result is important?

No theorem — and this matters, because tabular Q-learning *does* have a convergence proof, and it is
the function approximation that breaks it. Baird's counterexample shows linear-approximation
Q-learning can diverge. The paper's contribution is empirical stabilisation, not a fix to the theory.

## What proof techniques are used?

None. Ablations establish that both replay and the target network are individually necessary.

## What prior mathematics is required?

Markov decision processes, the Bellman optimality operator and its contraction property in the
tabular case, and why that contraction fails under projection onto a function class.

## Limitations

The max in the target produces overestimation bias (addressed by Double DQN); sample efficiency is
very poor ($10^7$ frames); no convergence guarantee; performance is uneven across games and fails
entirely on those needing long-horizon exploration.

## Why it mattered

It made deep reinforcement learning a field. The two tricks — replay and target networks — are still
standard, and the paper is the template for "the theory says this diverges; here is an engineering
intervention that makes it work", which describes a great deal of modern practice.
