## What problem does this paper solve?

Representation learning for tasks where the full input is available — classification, tagging,
retrieval, question answering over a given passage.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

The masked language modelling objective: replace a random $15\%$ of tokens with `[MASK]` and predict
them,
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{MLM}} = -\E\left[\sum_{t\in M}\log q_\theta\big(x_t\mid x_{\setminus M}\big)\right],
$$
where $M$ is the mask set. Unlike the
<TopicRef id="llm/language-modelling-and-cross-entropy">autoregressive factorisation</TopicRef>, this
is **not** a valid factorisation of the joint: the masked positions are predicted independently given
the context, so the model does not define a distribution over sequences.

## What assumptions are made?

That conditional independence of the masked positions given the unmasked ones is an acceptable
approximation — it is, for representation learning, and it is not, for generation, which is why BERT
cannot generate text.

## What equations matter?

The comparison with the autoregressive objective is the substantive point. Autoregressive:
$\log p(x) = \sum_t\log p(x_t\mid x_{<t})$, exact. MLM: an objective that trains a good encoder but
is a pseudo-likelihood, not a likelihood.

## What theorem or result is important?

No theorem. State-of-the-art on eleven NLP benchmarks, with the important observation that a single
pretrained model plus a light task-specific head beat task-specific architectures across the board.

## What proof techniques are used?

None.

## What prior mathematics is required?

Cross-entropy and conditional independence; the transformer architecture; the distinction between a
likelihood and a pseudo-likelihood.

## Limitations

Cannot generate. The `[MASK]` token appears in pretraining and never at fine-tuning time, a
train/test mismatch that later models addressed. Only $15\%$ of tokens supply a training signal per
example, making pretraining less sample-efficient per token than autoregressive training. And the
next-sentence-prediction auxiliary task was later shown to be unhelpful.

## Why it mattered

It established pretrain-then-fine-tune as the dominant paradigm and made transfer learning routine in
NLP. Its encoders remain the basis of most
<TopicRef id="rag/embedding-geometry-and-similarity">retrieval embedding models</TopicRef>, where
bidirectional context is exactly what is wanted.
