## What problem does this paper solve?

Scalable approximate inference in latent-variable models with flexible, neural likelihoods.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

The **evidence lower bound** with an amortised variational posterior $q_\phi(z\mid x)$:
$$
\log p_\theta(x) \ge \E_{q_\phi(z|x)}\big[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)\big] - \mathrm{KL}\big(q_\phi(z\mid x)\,\|\,p(z)\big) =: \mathcal{F}(\theta,\phi;x),
$$
and the reparameterisation $z = \mu_\phi(x)+\sigma_\phi(x)\odot\eps$, $\eps\sim\Ncal(0,I)$.

## What assumptions are made?

A differentiable, reparameterisable variational family — Gaussian in practice. Discrete latents are
not covered, which is why they need score-function estimators or relaxations.

## What equations matter?

The reparameterisation is the whole idea. With $z\sim q_\phi$ directly,
$\nabla_\phi\E_{q_\phi}[f(z)]$ requires the score-function estimator
$\E_{q_\phi}[f(z)\nabla_\phi\log q_\phi(z)]$, whose variance is large. After reparameterisation,
$$
\nabla_\phi\E_{\eps}\big[f(\mu_\phi+\sigma_\phi\odot\eps)\big] = \E_\eps\big[\nabla_\phi f(\mu_\phi+\sigma_\phi\odot\eps)\big],
$$
an ordinary pathwise derivative — one sample often suffices. The KL term is closed-form for Gaussians.

## What theorem or result is important?

The ELBO's gap is exactly $\mathrm{KL}(q_\phi(z\mid x)\|p_\theta(z\mid x))$, so maximising it both
fits the model and tightens the posterior approximation. The bound and the gap identity are the
theorem; the same computation appears in the
<TopicRef id="rag/retrieval-as-latent-variable-model">RAG variational bound</TopicRef>.

## What proof techniques are used?

Jensen's inequality for the bound; the change-of-variables identity for the estimator.

## What prior mathematics is required?

KL divergence, Jensen's inequality, change of variables for expectations, and Monte Carlo variance.

## Limitations

The Gaussian posterior is often too restrictive, producing blurry reconstructions. Posterior collapse
— the KL term driving $q_\phi(z\mid x)$ to the prior so the latent is unused — is a standard failure.
And the ELBO is a bound: a better ELBO does not necessarily mean a better model.

## Why it mattered

The reparameterisation trick is now used far beyond VAEs — in Bayesian neural networks, in
policy-gradient variance reduction, in normalising flows and in diffusion models. It is one of the
most broadly useful single ideas in the modern toolbox.
