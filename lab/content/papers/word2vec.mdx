## What problem does this paper solve?

Scalable learning of dense word representations, and the removal of the expensive softmax over a
large vocabulary.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

Skip-gram with **negative sampling**: for a centre word $w$ and context $c$,
$$
\mathcal{L} = -\log\sigma\big(\ip{v_w}{u_c}\big) - \sum_{j=1}^k\E_{c_j\sim P_n}\log\sigma\big(-\ip{v_w}{u_{c_j}}\big),
$$
with $P_n$ the unigram distribution raised to the power $3/4$.

## What assumptions are made?

That the $3/4$-power noise distribution is a good sampler, and that $k\approx5$–$20$ negatives suffice
to approximate the full softmax. Both are empirical choices that worked and were never derived.

## What equations matter?

Levy and Goldberg's subsequent analysis is the mathematically satisfying part: at the optimum, for
sufficiently high dimension, skip-gram with negative sampling implicitly factorises the **shifted
pointwise mutual information** matrix,
$$
\ip{v_w}{u_c} = \mathrm{PMI}(w,c) - \log k,
\qquad
\mathrm{PMI}(w,c) = \log\frac{p(w,c)}{p(w)p(c)} .
$$
So the method is matrix factorisation of a co-occurrence statistic, with the noise ratio $k$ acting as
a threshold.

## What theorem or result is important?

The PMI identification above (Levy–Goldberg, 2014), not in the original paper. It converts an
algorithm into an objective with a closed-form optimum, which is exactly the kind of result worth
looking for whenever a method is defined procedurally.

## What proof techniques are used?

In the original, none. The PMI result comes from setting the derivative of the objective with respect
to each inner product to zero and solving.

## What prior mathematics is required?

The logistic loss and its gradient; pointwise mutual information; low-rank matrix factorisation.

## Limitations

One vector per word type, so polysemy is unresolvable — the fix required contextual embeddings.
The famous analogy arithmetic ($\textit{king}-\textit{man}+\textit{woman}\approx\textit{queen}$) is
weaker than commonly reported, and depends on excluding the query words from the nearest-neighbour
search. Trained on co-occurrence alone, the embeddings encode social biases present in the corpus.

## Why it mattered

It made dense embeddings standard and is the direct ancestor of every
<TopicRef id="rag/embedding-geometry-and-similarity">retrieval embedding</TopicRef> in use. The
negative-sampling contrastive objective is also the ancestor of the in-batch-negatives training used
by <TopicRef id="rag/vector-search-and-ann">DPR</TopicRef> and by modern sentence encoders.
