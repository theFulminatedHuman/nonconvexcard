## What problem does this paper solve?

ImageNet classification with $1000$ classes and $1.2$M training images. Prior systems used
hand-engineered descriptors with shallow classifiers and had stalled around $26\%$ top-5 error.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

Nothing new mathematically. The paper's contribution is a *combination*: eight learned layers of
<TopicRef id="deep-learning/convolution-recurrence-normalisation">convolution</TopicRef> and pooling,
the ReLU nonlinearity $\rho(t) = \max(0,t)$, dropout as a stochastic regulariser, and
label-preserving data augmentation.

## What assumptions are made?

Translation equivariance of the relevant features, which is what makes convolution the right linear
map — and, by the characterisation theorem, the *only* translation-equivariant linear map. Locality:
that features are built from small spatial neighbourhoods.

## What equations matter?

$\rho(t) = \max(0,t)$, with derivative $\one\{t>0\}$: unlike the sigmoid, the derivative does not
decay for large positive input, so the
<TopicRef id="deep-learning/approximation-and-backpropagation">Jacobian product</TopicRef> across
layers does not shrink from saturation. The paper measured a six-fold training speed-up from this
change alone.

Dropout, at rate $p$: multiply activations by independent Bernoulli$(1-p)$ masks at training time.
The expected activation is preserved by rescaling, and the procedure can be read as training an
implicit ensemble of $2^{\#\text{units}}$ subnetworks with shared weights.

## What theorem or result is important?

Top-5 error of $15.3\%$ against $26.2\%$ for the next best entry — a margin large enough to end the
argument about whether learned features could beat designed ones.

## What proof techniques are used?

None. The paper is entirely empirical, with careful ablations showing that removing any single layer
degrades performance.

## What prior mathematics is required?

Convolution and its equivariance property; backpropagation on a computational graph; SGD with
momentum and weight decay.

## Limitations

No theory for any of the choices. The architecture was found by search and intuition. The local
response normalisation layer was later shown to be unnecessary and was replaced by
<TopicRef id="deep-learning/convolution-recurrence-normalisation">batch normalisation</TopicRef>.
Depth was limited to eight layers by optimisation difficulty, a barrier that
<TopicRef id="deep-learning/approximation-and-backpropagation">residual connections</TopicRef> removed.

## Why it mattered

It converted a research position into an engineering fact and redirected the field. The specific
technical lessons — ReLU for gradient flow, aggressive augmentation, GPU training — became standard
within two years.
