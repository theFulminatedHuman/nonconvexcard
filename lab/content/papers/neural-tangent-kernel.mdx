## What problem does this paper solve?

It supplies a regime in which training dynamics can be computed exactly, turning a question about
non-convex optimisation into a question about a linear system.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

The **neural tangent kernel**
$$
\Theta^{(L)}(x,x') = \ip{\nabla_\theta f(x;\theta)}{\nabla_\theta f(x';\theta)} ,
$$
and its infinite-width limit, which the paper shows is deterministic (independent of the random
initialisation) and computable by an explicit layerwise recursion.

## What assumptions are made?

The NTK parameterisation (each layer's output scaled by $1/\sqrt{\text{width}}$), widths tending to
infinity in all layers, and gradient flow rather than discrete steps. The scaling is essential and is
not the one used in practice.

## What equations matter?

Under gradient flow the network outputs on the training set evolve as
$$
\dot u_t = -\Theta\,(u_t - y),
$$
a linear ODE. Since $\Theta\succeq0$ this converges, and the rate is governed by
$\lambda_{\min}(\Theta)$ — which is exactly a
<TopicRef id="optimization/convex-sets-and-functions">PL inequality</TopicRef>, as the
<TopicRef id="deep-learning-theory/what-is-proven">theory page</TopicRef> shows.

## What theorem or result is important?

As width $\to\infty$: (i) $\Theta$ converges in probability to a deterministic limit; (ii) $\Theta$
stays constant during training; (iii) consequently the trained network equals kernel regression with
kernel $\Theta$. Du et al. and others subsequently turned this into finite-width convergence theorems
with explicit width requirements.

## What proof techniques are used?

Law-of-large-numbers arguments over the width for each layer, with a careful induction on depth to
control the recursion; concentration of the Gram matrices of the intermediate representations.

## What prior mathematics is required?

Kernels and RKHS; concentration for sums of independent random vectors; gradient flow as an ODE.

## Limitations

The regime is **lazy**: parameters move by $O(1/\sqrt{\text{width}})$, so no features are learned and
the "deep" structure is inert. The width requirements in the finite-width follow-ups are far beyond
practical models. Empirically the NTK kernel machine underperforms the finite trained network, and the
kernel is observed to drift substantially in real training. So the theory describes a regime that
exists mathematically and is not the regime practitioners are in.

## Why it mattered

It is the first setting in which deep-network training dynamics were solved exactly, and it made
precise what overparameterisation buys and what it does not. It also framed the central question
sharply: the interesting behaviour of real networks is *outside* the lazy regime, and the mean-field
and $\mu$P lines of work exist to reach it.
