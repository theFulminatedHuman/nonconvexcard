## What problem does this paper solve?

Predictability. Before this work, whether a $10\times$ larger model would be better was an empirical
question requiring the experiment. The paper turns it into an extrapolation.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

Power-law fits
$$
\mathcal{L}(N)\approx\left(\frac{N_c}{N}\right)^{\alpha_N},
\quad
\mathcal{L}(D)\approx\left(\frac{D_c}{D}\right)^{\alpha_D},
\quad
\mathcal{L}(C)\approx\left(\frac{C_c}{C}\right)^{\alpha_C},
$$
in non-embedding parameters, tokens and compute, together with a joint form interpolating between the
parameter- and data-limited regimes.

## What assumptions are made?

That the fitted functional form extrapolates; that the learning-rate schedule and other
hyperparameters are handled consistently across scales. The second assumption is where the analysis
was later found wanting.

## What equations matter?

The joint fit and the compute-optimal allocation derived from it. The paper's conclusion was
$N^\star\propto C^{0.73}$, $D^\star\propto C^{0.27}$ — strongly favouring larger models over more data.
The Lagrange computation behind such a conclusion is on the
<TopicRef id="llm/scaling-and-in-context-learning">scaling page</TopicRef>.

## What theorem or result is important?

No theorem. The empirical findings: smooth power laws with no visible breaks; weak dependence on
depth, width and aspect ratio at fixed parameter count; larger models are more sample-efficient.

## What proof techniques are used?

Curve fitting across a systematically varied grid of runs.

## What prior mathematics is required?

Log–log regression; constrained optimisation for the allocation rule; the
<TopicRef id="llm/language-modelling-and-cross-entropy">entropy floor</TopicRef> to interpret the
irreducible term.

## Limitations

The compute-optimal conclusion was **superseded**: Hoffmann et al. showed the fit was biased by the
use of a fixed learning-rate schedule that penalised runs trained on more tokens, and refitting gives
$N^\star,D^\star\propto C^{1/2}$ — an order-of-magnitude different recommendation. The laws also
describe loss, not capability, and the exponents are tokeniser- and dataset-dependent.

## Why it mattered

It made large-scale training planable and is the intellectual basis for the decision to build very
large models. That its headline allocation recommendation was later corrected is part of the lesson:
the functional form has held up, the fitted constants were methodology-dependent, and the whole
enterprise remains an empirical extrapolation with no derivation.
