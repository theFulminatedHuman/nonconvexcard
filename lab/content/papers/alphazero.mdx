## What problem does this paper solve?

Whether human data is necessary, and whether the learning-plus-search recipe is domain-specific. The
answers were no and no.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

A single network $(p_\theta, v_\theta) = f_\theta(s)$ outputting both a policy and a value, trained on
the loss
$$
\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \big(z - v_\theta(s)\big)^2 - \pi^\top\log p_\theta(\cdot\mid s) + \lambda\norm\theta_2^2 ,
$$
where $z$ is the game outcome and $\pi$ is the **visit-count distribution** produced by MCTS at that
position.

## What assumptions are made?

Perfect information, a known simulator, self-play symmetry. Also, implicitly, that the search output
$\pi$ is a better policy than $p_\theta$ — the policy-improvement assumption on which the whole
bootstrap rests.

## What equations matter?

The training target $\pi\propto N(s,a)^{1/\tau}$ from the search, against which the raw network policy
is regressed. This is the crux: MCTS acts as a **policy improvement operator**, and training the
network to imitate its own search output is an approximate policy iteration. Formally it is the
generalised-policy-iteration schema: improve by search, evaluate by self-play, project back onto the
function class.

## What theorem or result is important?

No theorem for the neural version. Classical policy iteration on a finite MDP is guaranteed to
improve monotonically and converge; the projection onto a neural function class destroys that
guarantee, and no replacement exists. Empirically: surpassed AlphaGo Lee in three days of training and
AlphaGo Master in three weeks, from random weights.

## What proof techniques are used?

None.

## What prior mathematics is required?

Policy iteration and its convergence in the tabular case; MCTS; cross-entropy and squared loss.

## Limitations

Perfect information and a simulator remain required. Enormous compute ($5000$ TPUs for self-play).
No guarantee that the projected policy iteration improves — it is observed to, and could in principle
oscillate. The generalisation across board games is real but all three games share the perfect-
information two-player zero-sum structure.

## Why it mattered

It demonstrated that a general algorithm plus compute can exceed systems built on domain knowledge —
a result frequently cited as evidence for scale over hand-design. The specific technical idea, using
search output as a training target for the policy that guides the search, is the cleanest example of
self-improvement by amortising expensive computation into a network.
