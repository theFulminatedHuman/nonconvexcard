## What problem does this paper solve?

Search in a game tree too large to enumerate, where no good hand-designed evaluation function exists.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

A policy network $p_\sigma(a\mid s)$ (trained by supervised learning on human games, then improved by
self-play policy gradient), a value network $v_\theta(s)$ estimating the outcome, and their use inside
MCTS via the selection rule
$$
a_t = \argmax_a\left(Q(s_t,a) + u(s_t,a)\right),
\qquad
u(s,a) \propto \frac{p_\sigma(a\mid s)}{1+N(s,a)},
$$
where $N$ is the visit count.

## What assumptions are made?

Perfect information, deterministic dynamics and a known simulator. All three are essential and none
holds in most real problems, which is the main limit on how far the recipe transfers.

## What equations matter?

The selection rule above is a variant of UCB applied to trees. The exploration term
$u(s,a)$ decays as $1/(1+N)$, so the search concentrates on promising moves — and the
<TopicRef id="high-dimensional-probability/concentration-inequalities">concentration</TopicRef> theory
behind UCB is what justifies balancing an empirical mean against a count-based confidence width, even
though the tree setting breaks the i.i.d. assumptions the bandit analysis needs.

## What theorem or result is important?

No theorem about AlphaGo itself. UCT (UCB applied to trees) has asymptotic convergence results under
assumptions that the neural-network-guided version does not satisfy. The result is the match outcome:
$5$–$0$ against the European champion and $4$–$1$ against Lee Sedol.

## What proof techniques are used?

None in the paper.

## What prior mathematics is required?

Bandit algorithms and UCB; policy gradients; Monte Carlo estimation and its variance.

## Limitations

Requires a simulator and perfect information. Bootstrapped from human games, so it inherited human
biases — the limitation <TopicRef id="deep-learning/approximation-and-backpropagation">AlphaZero</TopicRef>
removed. Enormous compute. And the combination of learning and search is not analysed: why search
improves on the policy network, and by how much, is understood only heuristically as a policy
improvement operator.

## Why it mattered

It settled a long-standing benchmark a decade earlier than expected, and it established the
learning-plus-search pattern — a learned prior narrowing a search, the search generating better
targets for the prior — that recurs in program synthesis, theorem proving and current
inference-time-compute methods for language models.
