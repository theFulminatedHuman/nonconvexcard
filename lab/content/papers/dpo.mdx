## What problem does this paper solve?

Pipeline complexity. RLHF needs a reward model, a value model, on-policy sampling and PPO's clipping
machinery. DPO needs a loss function.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

The **implicit reward**
$$
\hat r_\theta(x,y) = \beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y\mid x)},
$$
which is the reward whose KL-regularised optimum is $\pi_\theta$, up to a function of $x$ alone.

## What assumptions are made?

Exactly those of RLHF: Bradley–Terry preferences, and the KL-regularised objective with the same
$\beta$ and reference policy. Nothing is added; nothing is removed.

## What equations matter?

The derivation, in full on the
<TopicRef id="llm/preference-optimisation">preference optimisation page</TopicRef>: invert
$\pi^\star\propto\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}e^{r/\beta}$ to get $r$ in terms of $\pi^\star$, substitute into
Bradley–Terry, and observe that the intractable partition function $Z(x)$ cancels because the
preference model uses only reward *differences* at fixed $x$. The result is
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}} = -\E\log\sigma\!\left(\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_w|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_w|x)} - \beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_l|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_l|x)}\right).
$$

## What theorem or result is important?

The equivalence itself: the set of optimal policies under the two objectives coincides. This is a
genuine theorem, proved by the change of variables, and it is exact rather than approximate.

## What proof techniques are used?

A Gibbs-measure characterisation of the KL-regularised optimum (a short variational argument), then
algebra. The whole proof is half a page.

## What prior mathematics is required?

KL divergence, the Gibbs variational principle, and the invariance of Bradley–Terry to
prompt-dependent reward shifts.

## Limitations

The equivalence holds at the optimum with unlimited data. Off the preference distribution the implicit
reward is unconstrained, so the optimised policy can drift where nothing pins it — RLHF's on-policy
sampling supplies a signal there and DPO does not. The Bradley–Terry assumption is inherited, not
removed. Empirically DPO can collapse output diversity more sharply and is sensitive to
$\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$'s quality.

## Why it mattered

It removed a large engineering barrier and became the default preference-tuning method within a year.
Mathematically it is a fine example of how far a correctly-chosen reparameterisation goes: no new
analysis, no new assumption, and a substantially simpler algorithm.
