## What problem does this paper solve?

It exhibits a clean, reproducible experimental handle on the transition from memorisation to
generalisation — a transition that in normal training is invisible because both happen together.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

None. The tasks are group operations: modular addition and multiplication, permutation composition,
and similar, presented as $a\circ b = c$ triples with a fraction of the multiplication table held out.

## What assumptions are made?

The phenomenon requires small datasets relative to the model, and its onset depends strongly on weight
decay — a fact the paper reports and which subsequent work has built explanations on.

## What equations matter?

None in the paper. The most convincing follow-up analysis (Nanda et al., mechanistic
interpretability) reverse-engineered the modular-addition circuit and found the network computes
$$
a+b \bmod p
$$
by embedding inputs on circles at several frequencies $\omega$, using trigonometric identities
$\cos\omega(a+b) = \cos\omega a\cos\omega b - \sin\omega a\sin\omega b$ realised by the attention and
MLP layers, and reading off the answer by constructive interference. That is a complete mechanistic
account — for that one task.

## What theorem or result is important?

No theorem. The empirical result: a delay of $10^2$–$10^4\times$ between fitting and generalising,
robust across tasks and seeds.

## What proof techniques are used?

None.

## What prior mathematics is required?

Nothing beyond the basics; the follow-up analyses use discrete Fourier analysis on $\Z_p$.

## Limitations

The evidence is from small models on synthetic algorithmic data. Whether grokking occurs in
large-scale training is unclear — related "emergence" phenomena have been argued to be artefacts of
discontinuous metrics rather than discontinuous capabilities. Several competing explanations
(memorisation-to-generalisation circuit competition, weight-norm dynamics, slow feature alignment)
each fit some experiments.

## Why it mattered

It separated two processes that are normally confounded, which made the memorisation-to-generalisation
transition experimentally accessible. It is also one of the few cases where a deep network's learned
algorithm has been fully identified, which is a proof of concept for mechanistic interpretability.
