## What problem does this paper solve?

The **degradation** problem. A $56$-layer plain network had higher *training* error than a $20$-layer
one on CIFAR-10. Since the deeper network's function class strictly contains the shallower one's — set
the extra layers to the identity — this is not a capacity or generalisation failure. It is a failure
to find a solution that provably exists.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

The residual block
$$
y = x + F(x;\theta),
$$
where $F$ is two or three convolution–normalisation–ReLU layers. The reparameterisation is exact: the
function class is unchanged, only the coordinates on it are different.

## What assumptions are made?

Implicitly, that the target function is closer to the identity than to zero in the relevant sense —
that each block needs to make a *small* adjustment. This is a hypothesis about the problem, and it is
why the reparameterisation helps rather than being a cosmetic rewrite.

## What equations matter?

The Jacobian of a residual block is
$$
\frac{\partial y}{\partial x} = I + \frac{\partial F}{\partial x},
$$
so a stack of $L$ blocks has Jacobian $\prod_\ell\big(I + \partial F_\ell/\partial x\big)$. When the
$\partial F_\ell$ are small this product stays near $I$, whereas the corresponding plain-network
product $\prod_\ell J_\ell$ is
<TopicRef id="deep-learning/approximation-and-backpropagation">generically exponential in $L$</TopicRef>.
This is the mathematical content of the paper.

## What theorem or result is important?

No theorem. Empirically: $152$-layer networks train successfully and win ImageNet 2015; the degradation
curve is removed; and a $1202$-layer network trains (though it overfits). The accompanying observation
that the learned residuals have small norm supports the assumption above.

## What proof techniques are used?

None in the original paper. Later work (the identity-mappings follow-up) analysed the forward and
backward signal propagation through the skip path more carefully, and the general phenomenon connects
to the <TopicRef id="deep-learning-theory/what-is-proven">landscape</TopicRef> literature.

## What prior mathematics is required?

The chain rule as a Jacobian product, operator norms, and the observation that identity-plus-small
matrices compose stably.

## Limitations

The explanation is a plausibility argument, not a theorem: there is no result stating that residual
parameterisation makes the optimisation problem easier in a precise sense. Very deep ResNets behave
in some respects like ensembles of shallower paths, which complicates the "depth is doing the work"
reading.

## Why it mattered

The additive skip connection is now in essentially every deep architecture, including every
<TopicRef id="deep-learning/transformers">transformer</TopicRef>, where the residual stream is the
central object. The paper also established the diagnostic discipline of distinguishing optimisation
failure from generalisation failure by looking at training error.
