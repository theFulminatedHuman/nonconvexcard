## What problem does this paper solve?

An RNN computes $h_t$ from $h_{t-1}$, so the $T$ steps of a sequence must be executed in order. On
hardware whose throughput comes from parallelism this is a hard constraint, and it interacts badly
with the <TopicRef id="deep-learning/convolution-recurrence-normalisation">vanishing-gradient
product</TopicRef>: information from position $1$ to position $T$ traverses $T$ multiplications.
Convolutional sequence models parallelise but need $O(\log_k T)$ stacked layers to connect distant
positions.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

Scaled dot-product attention as a primitive,
$$
\operatorname{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V,
$$
multi-head attention as $h$ parallel low-rank instances of it, and sinusoidal positional encodings to
restore the order information that attention's
<TopicRef id="deep-learning/transformers">permutation equivariance</TopicRef> destroys.

## What assumptions are made?

That the required interactions between positions can be expressed as a small number of
content-addressed soft lookups per layer, and that $O(T^2)$ compute in the sequence length is
acceptable. The second assumption is what long-context work has since had to revisit.

## What equations matter?

The attention formula above, the $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ scaling (justified in the paper by exactly the
variance argument on the
<TopicRef id="deep-learning/transformers">transformers page</TopicRef>), and the complexity table
comparing $O(T^2d)$ for self-attention against $O(Td^2)$ for recurrence — the paper's central
engineering claim is that the former has $O(1)$ sequential operations while the latter has $O(T)$.

## What theorem or result is important?

There is no theorem. The paper's results are empirical: state-of-the-art BLEU on WMT translation at a
fraction of the training cost, plus ablations on head count, key dimension and positional encoding
scheme. It is worth being clear that the transformer's justification was, and largely remains,
experimental.

## What proof techniques are used?

None. The only analysis is the variance calculation motivating the scaling factor.

## What prior mathematics is required?

Linear algebra for the low-rank structure of heads, softmax calculus for the gradient behaviour, and
the <TopicRef id="deep-learning/approximation-and-backpropagation">Jacobian-product</TopicRef> view of
depth to see why the residual connections are load-bearing.

## Limitations

Quadratic cost in sequence length; no theory for why attention is the right inductive bias; the
original post-norm placement needed learning-rate warmup and was later replaced by pre-norm; absolute
sinusoidal encodings extrapolate poorly and have been superseded by relative and rotary schemes.

## Why it mattered

Every large language model in use descends from this architecture. The specific reason is the one the
paper emphasised least: making the per-layer computation a dense matrix multiplication over the whole
sequence turned sequence modelling into a problem that scales with hardware, which is the
precondition for the <TopicRef id="deep-learning-theory/what-is-proven">scaling-law</TopicRef> regime.
