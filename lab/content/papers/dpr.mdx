## What problem does this paper solve?

The vocabulary-mismatch failure of lexical retrieval, and the practical question of how to train a
dense retriever without prohibitive negative-sampling cost.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

Two encoders $f_Q,f_Z$ and the similarity $s(q,z) = \ip{f_Q(q)}{f_Z(z)}$, trained with the
softmax contrastive loss over a positive and $n$ negatives:
$$
\mathcal{L} = -\log\frac{e^{s(q,z^+)}}{e^{s(q,z^+)}+\sum_{j=1}^n e^{s(q,z_j^-)}} .
$$

## What assumptions are made?

That other passages in the same batch are valid negatives for a given question. This is the
**in-batch negatives** trick: with batch size $B$ it supplies $B-1$ negatives at no extra encoder
cost, since every passage in the batch must be encoded anyway. The assumption fails when the batch
contains a false negative — a passage that does in fact answer the question.

## What equations matter?

The contrastive loss above is exactly a
<TopicRef id="machine-learning/logistic-regression">softmax classification</TopicRef> over the batch,
so its gradient is the familiar prediction-minus-target form. The loss also has a direct geometric
reading: it pushes $f_Q(q)$ towards $f_Z(z^+)$ and away from the other directions, which is the
mechanism that counteracts the
<TopicRef id="rag/embedding-geometry-and-similarity">anisotropy</TopicRef> of raw pretrained
encoders.

## What theorem or result is important?

No theorem. Top-20 retrieval accuracy improved from $59\%$ (BM25) to $79\%$ on Natural Questions,
and the downstream QA accuracy improved correspondingly.

## What proof techniques are used?

None; ablations on negative selection (random, BM25-hard, in-batch) establish that hard negatives
matter most.

## What prior mathematics is required?

Inner-product similarity and normalisation; the softmax and cross-entropy gradient;
approximate nearest-neighbour search for the index.

## Limitations

The bi-encoder computes no query–document interaction, so it cannot express matching that requires
attending jointly — which is why a cross-encoder reranker is added on top, at the cost of the
<TopicRef id="rag/vector-search-and-ann">recall ceiling</TopicRef> argument applying. Training is
sensitive to negative mining. And the index must be rebuilt whenever $f_Z$ changes, which is why
end-to-end retriever training is awkward.

## Why it mattered

It established the dual-encoder-with-in-batch-negatives recipe that every production embedding model
still uses, and it made dense retrieval the default rather than a research curiosity.
