## What problem does this paper solve?

**Objective mismatch.** Maximum likelihood on web text optimises
$\mathrm{KL}(\hat p\,\|\,q_\theta)$ against the empirical text distribution, which is not the
objective "be helpful and follow the instruction". The paper's contribution is a way to optimise
something closer to the latter.

## What mathematical objects are introduced?

A reward model $r_\phi(x,y)$ fit by
<TopicRef id="llm/preference-optimisation">Bradley–Terry</TopicRef> maximum likelihood on preference
pairs, and the KL-regularised RL objective
$$
\max_\pi\ \E_{x,y\sim\pi}\big[r_\phi(x,y)\big] - \beta\,\mathrm{KL}\big(\pi\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}\big) + \gamma\,\E_{\text{pretrain}}\big[\log\pi\big],
$$
where the last term mitigates capability regression on the pretraining distribution.

## What assumptions are made?

That human preferences are generated by a single latent scalar reward with a logistic link
(Bradley–Terry), that the labelers' preferences represent the intended objective, and that the KL
budget keeps the policy in the region where $r_\phi$ is accurate.

## What equations matter?

The Bradley–Terry likelihood and the KL-regularised objective above, whose optimum has the
<TopicRef id="llm/preference-optimisation">closed form</TopicRef>
$\pi^\star\propto\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}e^{r/\beta}$ — intractable to compute directly, which is why PPO is
used.

## What theorem or result is important?

No theorem. The headline result: outputs of the $1.3$B InstructGPT model were preferred by human
labelers to those of the $175$B GPT-3 base model, a $100\times$ effective parameter saving from
alignment alone.

## What proof techniques are used?

None; the pipeline is engineering and the evaluation is human preference judgment.

## What prior mathematics is required?

KL divergence and its variational properties; policy-gradient methods; the
<TopicRef id="llm/preference-optimisation">identifiability</TopicRef> of Bradley–Terry rewards.

## Limitations

Reward hacking as $\beta\to0$, with no bound on the true reward of the optimised policy in terms of
$r_\phi$'s error. An "alignment tax" — degradation on some capability benchmarks. Dependence on
labeler demographics and instructions, which the paper is explicit about. And the complexity of the
pipeline (three models, an RL loop) which motivated <TopicRef id="llm/preference-optimisation">DPO</TopicRef>.

## Why it mattered

It is the paper that made language models usable as assistants, and every deployed chat model
descends from this recipe. It also established preference data as the central training resource for
the final stage of model development.
